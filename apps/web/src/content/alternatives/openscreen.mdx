## What is OpenScreen, and what is it good at?

OpenScreen is a free, MIT-licensed screen recorder and editor for macOS, Windows
and Linux, and it is unusually complete for a project that costs nothing. Zooms
are placed from your clicks by a fixed rule with no network call. Each zoom can
stay flat or turn the screen in 3D with depth of field on the far side. The
pointer is recorded apart from the pixels, so its size and style can change
afterwards, and there are five cursor packs to change them to. Backgrounds,
window and device frames, four camera layouts, a local segmentation model that
cuts you out of the room, and captions from whisper.cpp running on your own
machine.

Export is MP4 from 720p up to the source size at 24, 30 or 60 frames a second in
H.264, or a GIF. There are no paid tiers and no watermark.

## Why do people look for an OpenScreen alternative?

**It is an Electron app doing a native job.** That shows up in export time, in
memory, and in how the app behaves next to everything else on a Mac.

**Thirty, sixty, and that is the ceiling.** 60 frames a second at the source
size is where it stops, and H.264 is the only codec out.

**The pace is a maintainer's pace.** A free project ships when its author has an
evening, and support is an issue tracker.

## Is Prequel a good OpenScreen alternative?

If the recording is work, yes.

Prequel captures through ScreenCaptureKit and encodes through the Mac's own
media engine, which is what 4K at 120 frames a second needs. Zooms land on every
click and every burst of typing, tilted, with focus falling away from whatever
they are aimed at. The camera is framed, shaped and cut out of its background.
Seventeen colour looks sit over the picture, a loupe magnifies the work under
the pointer, and Apple's sound isolation cleans the microphone. Captions are
transcribed on device by Apple's own speech framework, with no model to
download.

It is $9 a month or $39 once, with a seven-day trial.

## What do you give up by switching?

Free, MIT, Windows and Linux, and the 3D camera move on a zoom, which is
OpenScreen's own and is genuinely good. Prequel tilts the push-in and pulls
focus; it does not orbit the screen.

If the requirement is software you can read, fork and run anywhere, OpenScreen
is the honest answer and this page is not arguing otherwise.
