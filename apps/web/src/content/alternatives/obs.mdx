## What is OBS Studio, and what is it good at?

OBS Studio is the free, open-source broadcast desk the whole streaming world
runs on. You build scenes out of sources (a display, a window, a camera, a
browser, an image, text), switch between them with transitions and hotkeys, mix
every audio source with filters like noise suppression and a gate, and send the
result to a file or to Twitch, YouTube and about thirty other destinations.

It runs on Windows, macOS 13 or later and Linux, it is GPL-licensed, and it has
a plugin API with years of work built on top of it. For anything live, anything
with multiple cameras, or anything that has to be free and inspectable, OBS is
the answer and nothing here changes that.

## Why do people look for an OBS alternative?

Because a recorded demo is not a broadcast, and OBS asks you to set up a
broadcast either way.

**Everything is manual, before and after.** Canvas size, output resolution,
encoder, bitrate, keyframe interval, audio tracks: all of it is yours to choose
before the first take. The file that comes out is exactly what was on screen,
at one distance, with a system cursor in the middle of it. Zooms, framing and
cuts happen in a video editor afterwards.

**It is a lot of app for a two-minute walkthrough.** The Apple Silicon build is
188 MB, the window is four docks of controls, and the common path for a demo is
record in OBS, then open the file in something else to make it watchable.

## Is Prequel a good OBS alternative?

For recording your own screen, yes. For streaming, no.

Prequel captures the screen, the camera, the microphone and system audio as
separate tracks and opens the editor with the pass already made: zooms on every
click and every burst of typing, tilted, with focus falling away from the
subject, the camera framed over a background, the cursor smoothed. There is
nothing to configure before recording and nothing to import afterwards.

It exports one file at up to 4K and 120 frames a second through the Mac's own
media engine, at $9 a month or $29 once, with a seven-day trial.

## What do you give up by switching?

Live streaming, entirely. No RTMP, no scenes, no transitions, no multiview, no
plugin API, no virtual camera. If you go live, OBS stays on your Mac and that
is the right outcome.

Also free, open source and cross-platform. OBS costs nothing, the source is
there to read, and it runs on Windows and Linux as well as macOS 13 and Intel
Macs. Prequel is a paid app on Apple Silicon and macOS 14 or later.
