## What is OBS, and what is it good at?

OBS Studio is free, open source, and the most capable video compositor most
people will ever install. You build scenes out of sources — a display, a window,
a camera, a browser, an image — position and filter each one, point an encoder
at the result, and record or stream it. It runs on macOS, Windows and Linux, has
no account, no limits and no watermark, and it is funded by donations.

For live streaming it is not a compromise, it is the tool. Nothing else on this
page streams at all. And because you configure the encoder yourself, there is no
product-imposed ceiling on resolution or bitrate — it records whatever your
machine can sustain.

If you already have an OBS setup you are happy with, keep it.

## Why do people look for an OBS alternative on Mac?

Because a scene graph is a strange thing to build before recording a two-minute
demo.

OBS asks you to make every decision up front. Which sources, at what size, in
what order, with which filters, at what canvas resolution, through which
encoder. Get it wrong and you find out afterwards. Change your mind about where
the webcam sits and you are editing the scene, not the video.

And then it stops. OBS records; it does not edit. There is no timeline, no
trimming, no zoom, no cursor work. Everything that turns a capture into
something watchable happens in a separate application afterwards — which is
exactly the afternoon in an editor that most people never actually spend.

One thing to correct, because most comparison pages still get it wrong: **OBS no
longer needs BlackHole to record system audio on a Mac.** OBS 30 and later on
macOS 13 and later ship a macOS Audio Capture source that records desktop audio
directly, either all of it or one application. The virtual-driver advice is
years out of date, and repeating it is how a page loses its credibility with the
people most likely to read it.

## Is Prequel a good OBS alternative?

For recording a Mac screen and getting a finished video out, yes — and the
difference is entirely about when decisions get made.

Prequel asks one question before recording: which screen, window or region.
Everything else is decided afterwards, on a take you can already see. Where the
camera sits, what shape it is, whether it appears at all, what background is
behind the recording, where the zooms go, how fast they move, where the cuts
land. None of it has to be right before you press record, because none of it is
burned in while you do.

The zooms are the part OBS has no answer to. Prequel records where you click and
what you type, clusters those moments, and opens the editor with the pushes
already placed. In OBS that is manual keyframing in another application.

## What do you give up by switching?

Quite a lot, honestly.

**Streaming.** Prequel does not stream. If any part of your work is live, OBS
stays installed.

**Configurability.** Filters, chroma key, a dozen simultaneous sources, capture
cards, custom encoder settings, plugins — OBS does all of it and Prequel does
none of it. Prequel is opinionated on purpose, and opinionated means there are
things you cannot express.

**Windows and Linux.** OBS runs everywhere. Prequel is macOS on Apple Silicon.

**Free forever.** OBS costs nothing, ever, and is open source. Prequel has a free
tier but the full version is a purchase.

The trade is narrow and worth stating plainly: you give up control and get back
the hour afterwards.
