## What is Screen Studio, and what is it good at?

Screen Studio is a macOS screen recorder that made this category. It records
your screen, watches where your cursor goes, and turns that into zooms and
smooth cursor movement automatically — so a plain walkthrough comes out looking
like somebody edited it. It also frames a webcam, puts the recording on a
background, records system audio, exports up to 4K 60, and records an iPhone or
iPad over USB with the device frame drawn in.

It is a mature, well-made product from a small team, and it is genuinely the
thing most people mean when they say they want a video that looks produced. If
you have never used anything in this category, Screen Studio is the reference
point — including for us.

## Why do people look for a Screen Studio alternative?

Overwhelmingly one reason: it is a subscription, and it did not used to be.

Screen Studio sold a one-time licence until October 2025, when it was withdrawn
for new customers. Existing holders keep their version and were given updates
into 2027, but anyone arriving today has two options: $29 a month billed
monthly, or $9 a month billed yearly — $108 a year. There is no free tier.

That is the friction. A screen recorder is a tool people open a few times a
month, it runs entirely on their own machine, and paying rent for it feels
different from paying rent for something with servers behind it. The founder
has been unusually candid about the reaction, saying publicly that he regrets
how the change was handled.

Worth being fair about the number, because most comparison pages are not: **$9
a month billed yearly is not expensive.** If you record regularly, Screen Studio
is good value, and the objection is the shape of the payment rather than the
size of it. Prequel is $59 a year — cheaper, but not so much cheaper that price
alone should move you if you are happy with what you have.

The second reason is scale. Screen Studio is built for short pieces, and long
takes are where people report trouble — a multi-hour recording producing an
enormous project, no export queue, and no editing while an export runs. If your
work is thirty-second demos, none of that will ever touch you.

## Is Prequel a good Screen Studio alternative?

For the core job, yes. Prequel records the screen, the webcam, the microphone
and system audio as separate tracks, watches where you click **and what you
type**, and opens an editor on the take with the zooms already placed, the
camera framed and a background applied. Same idea, same output shape.

Three concrete differences:

- **It costs about half as much.** $59 per user per year against Screen
  Studio's $108, with one plan rather than a monthly rate and a yearly rate to
  choose between, and everything in it — transcripts, uploads and shareable
  links included rather than sold separately.
- **It exports higher.** Up to 4K at 120 frames per second, against Screen
  Studio's 4K 60.
- **Typing drives zooms too.** A burst of typing is treated as a moment worth
  pushing into, not just a click — which matters if you record code or forms.

## What do you give up by switching?

Two things, and we would rather name them than have you find out.

**Screen Studio records iPhones and iPads. Prequel does not.** Plug a device in
and Screen Studio detects the model and colour and draws the frame. Prequel
captures a display, a window or a region of your Mac — nothing else. If mobile
demos are your work, that alone settles it.

**Screen Studio is finished and Prequel is not.** It has years of releases,
thousands of users and a support history. Prequel is in development. That is a
real difference and no feature table shows it.

If neither of those stops you, the argument is straightforward: the same job,
without the subscription.
