## What is Cap, and what is it good at?

Cap is an open-source screen recorder — AGPL v3, with the whole platform
self-hostable through Docker — and it is the closest thing on this page to what
Prequel does.

Its Studio Mode has smart auto-zoom that follows clicks, cursor smoothing and
click effects, custom backgrounds and rounded corners, separate control of
microphone and system audio levels, and 4K 60 export. It states plainly that all
recording and editing happen locally on your device, and it lets you connect
your own S3 bucket or Google Drive instead of its cloud. It runs on macOS and
Windows.

There is a free tier covering Studio Mode and 4K 60 export for personal use,
with shared links capped at five minutes. A desktop licence is $29 for a single
user, and Cap Pro is $12 per user per month or $8.16 billed annually.

That is a genuinely good product, honestly priced, and if open source is a
requirement for you then the rest of this page is academic — choose Cap.

## Where the usual argument does not work

Most comparisons on this site lean on two things: the recording never leaves
your machine, and there is no subscription. Neither is a difference here.

Cap processes locally and says so. Cap has a one-off licence tier. Cap is more
open than Prequel will ever be, because Prequel is not open source at all. A
comparison page that claimed "local-only" as a differentiator against Cap would
be wrong, and we would rather say that than hope you did not check.

Worth noting one oddity in their own pricing copy: the $29 desktop licence is
described as perpetual and as billed yearly in the same place. We are not going
to resolve that for them — check before buying.

## So why choose Prequel?

Three specific things, and they are narrow.

**It is macOS only, and built that way.** Capture is ScreenCaptureKit,
compositing is Metal, encoding is VideoToolbox, and the core is Rust. There is
no cross-platform abstraction in the middle, which is a real cost — no Windows
build, ever — bought for a narrow benefit.

**It exports higher.** Up to 4K at 120 frames per second, at a constant frame
rate, against Cap's 4K 60.

**Typing drives zooms.** Cap's auto-zoom follows clicks. Prequel also treats a
burst of typing as a moment worth pushing into, with a zoom target that follows
the text rather than the pointer. If you record code, terminals or forms, that
is the difference between a push that tracks your edit and one that sits on the
mouse.

## What do you give up by switching?

Windows, and the source code.

Cap runs on both platforms; Prequel does not and will not. Cap can be audited,
forked, self-hosted and kept running by you indefinitely; Prequel cannot. For a
lot of people that is decisive on its own, and it should be.

If neither matters to you and you are on a Mac, the argument narrows to export
ceiling, the typing-driven zooms, and which one you prefer using.
