## What is CleanShot X, and what is it good at?

CleanShot X is the best screenshot tool on the Mac, and it is not especially
close. Scrolling capture, annotation, background and shadow on a screenshot,
OCR, pinned overlays, a clean quick-access overlay, cloud links with a custom
domain, and a genuinely one-off price at $29 with an optional $19 a year if you
want to keep updating.

It also records video — screen, region or window, with system audio, out to MP4
or GIF, with a trim.

If you bought it, you almost certainly bought it for the screenshots, and this
page is not an argument against keeping it.

## Where its video stops

Deliberately early. CleanShot X's recording is built for the same job as its
screenshots: capture something, mark it up, get a link into a thread quickly.

So there is no automatic zoom, no cursor smoothing or resizing, no camera
composited into the frame, no background behind the recording, and no timeline
beyond trimming the ends. The polish CleanShot X is famous for is applied to
still images — a recording comes out as what was on the screen.

That is a reasonable place to stop for a tool that is mostly a screenshot app.
It only becomes a problem when the video has to go somewhere public.

## Is Prequel a good CleanShot X alternative?

For video specifically, yes — and the two overlap far less than the feature
lists suggest.

Prequel records the screen, the webcam, the microphone and system audio as
separate tracks, watches where you click and type, and opens an editor with
zooms already placed on those moments, the cursor cleaned up, the camera framed
and a background applied. Cuts happen on a real timeline with a waveform under
every clip. Export is hardware H.264 or HEVC at up to 4K and 120 frames per
second.

It also shares CleanShot X's pricing instinct, which is probably why you are
here: bought once, a year of updates, and the version you have keeps working
afterwards. There is a free tier on top of that, which CleanShot X does not
have.

## Should you replace it, or run both?

Run both, most likely.

Prequel does not take screenshots at all — no annotation, no scrolling capture,
no OCR, no pinning. Nothing here replaces the thing CleanShot X is actually
loved for, and a Mac with both installed is a completely sensible setup: one for
stills and quick clips, one for a recording that has to look produced.

The only reason to choose between them is if your video work is the reason you
were considering CleanShot X in the first place. In that case it is the lighter
half of a tool built for something else.
