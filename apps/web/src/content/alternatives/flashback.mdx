## What is FlashBack, and what is it good at?

FlashBack is a Windows screen recorder and editor from Blueberry Software, and
it has been going long enough to have earned its reputation in corporate
training. Record the screen, the camera and audio across multiple monitors, then
edit: cut, add callouts and arrows, blur anything private, insert zoom and pan,
and publish to a file or to a video platform.

The free Starter edition is unusually honest. No time limit, no watermark, and
it records screen, camera and audio across monitors. The paid editions are $39 a
year or $65 for a lifetime licence, both often sold at a discount, with a
Creator edition above them.

## Why do people look for a FlashBack alternative?

**It is Windows software.** FlashBack runs on Windows 10 and 11. There is no
Mac build, so switching platform means switching tool.

**Zoom and pan are something you place.** The editor has them, and every one is
a decision you make on a timeline after the fact. On a half-hour training video
that is an afternoon.

## Is Prequel a good FlashBack alternative?

On a Mac, for demos, tutorials and training clips, yes.

Prequel records the screen, the camera, the microphone and system audio as
separate tracks and opens the editor with the pass already made: zooms on every
click and every burst of typing, tilted, with focus falling away from whatever
the push-in is aimed at. The camera is framed, shaped and cut out of the room
behind you. A background goes behind the window. The cursor is smoothed and
hidden when it idles. Captions are transcribed on your Mac, and blur regions
cover anything private.

Export is one file at up to 4K and 120 frames a second, at $9 a month or $29
once, after a seven-day trial that exports without a watermark.

## What do you give up by switching?

Windows, and a genuinely capable free tier. FlashBack Starter records with no
watermark and no time limit, which is more than most free recorders allow, and
Prequel is a paid app after seven days.

Also the training-video furniture: callouts, arrows and annotation drawn on top
of the picture. Prequel frames, zooms, cuts and captions, and leaves the
annotation out.
