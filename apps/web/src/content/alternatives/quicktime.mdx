## What is QuickTime Player, and what is it good at?

QuickTime Player is already on your Mac. File > New Screen Recording, pick a
microphone, press record, and a `.mov` lands on the desktop. On recent versions
of macOS that menu item hands you over to the Screenshot app, which is the same
recorder with a toolbar: whole screen, one window, or a dragged region, a timer,
and a setting that draws a black circle round the pointer when you click.

Nothing to install, nothing to pay for, nothing to learn. For a bug report, a
clip to send a colleague, or a recording somebody else is going to edit, it is
the right tool and there is no reason to open anything heavier.

## Why do people look for a QuickTime alternative?

Because the file it gives you is the take you performed, and nothing else.

**There is no camera and no editing.** QuickTime records the screen with a
microphone. It cannot put your webcam in the corner, it holds one distance for
the whole take, and the only edit it offers afterwards is a trim at either end.
Every zoom, every cut and every background is somebody opening a second app.

**System audio needs a workaround.** The sound the Mac itself makes is not in
the recorder below macOS 27, so recording a video call or an app's own audio
means installing a virtual audio driver and routing output through it.

## Is Prequel a good QuickTime alternative?

Yes, and it is the same keystroke to start.

Prequel records the screen, the camera, the microphone and system audio as
separate tracks, with no driver to install, then opens the editor with the first
pass already made. Zooms are placed on every click and every burst of typing,
tilted, with focus falling away from whatever the push-in is aimed at. The
camera is framed and shaped afterwards. A background goes behind the recording
with padding and a shadow. The cursor is smoothed, blurred as it moves and
hidden when it idles. Captions are transcribed on your Mac.

Export is one MP4 or HEVC file at up to 4K and 120 frames a second, encoded by
the Mac's own media engine. It costs $9 a month, or $29 once, after a seven-day
trial with the export in it.

## What do you give up by switching?

Free, and already installed. QuickTime costs nothing and is on every Mac,
including Intel ones and every version of macOS back years. Prequel needs an
Apple Silicon Mac on macOS 14 or later, and after the trial it is a purchase.

QuickTime also plays and inspects video, records audio on its own, and captures
an iPhone over USB. Prequel records a display, a window or a region, and nothing
else.
