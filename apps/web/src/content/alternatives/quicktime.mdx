## What is QuickTime good at?

It is already there. No download, no account, no licence, no update prompt — a
keyboard shortcut and you are recording at your display's native resolution,
which on a Retina Mac is a genuinely high-quality capture. Stop, and you have a
plain `.mov` that belongs to you and opens anywhere.

For "capture this and send it to somebody", nothing is faster. QuickTime will
also trim, split, rotate and rearrange clips, which covers more than people
expect.

Most screen recordings do not need more than this. It is worth being honest
about that before arguing for anything else.

## Where QuickTime stops

Three places, and the first one catches almost everybody.

**System audio.** Apple's own documentation for a screen recording lists exactly
one audio option, and it is a microphone. There is no setting for the sound your
Mac is making. This is why the standard advice on every forum is to install a
virtual audio driver like BlackHole and route your output through it — then
remember to route it back, or your speakers stay broken. It is a kernel-adjacent
audio driver installed to work around a missing checkbox.

**Your camera and your screen in one frame.** QuickTime records a screen, or a
camera. Putting both in one video means recording twice and compositing them
yourself somewhere else.

**Everything that makes a recording watchable.** No zoom, so the thing you are
demonstrating stays as small as it was on your screen. No cursor work, so the
pointer skids around and sits on top of whatever you just explained. No
background, no padding, no way to reframe for vertical.

There is also the file size. QuickTime writes a MOV tuned for quality rather
than size, which is why a five-minute recording is startlingly large.

## Is Prequel a good QuickTime alternative?

It is built for exactly the gap above.

Prequel captures the screen, the webcam, the microphone and system audio at the
same time, as four separate tracks — no virtual driver, nothing to install, and
nothing to switch back afterwards. Because the camera is never burned into the
screen recording, its shape, size and corner are decided after the take rather
than before it, and it can come and go partway through.

While it records it watches where you click and type, and opens an editor with
zooms already placed on those moments, the cursor smoothed and resized, and a
background applied. Export is hardware H.264 or HEVC at up to 4K and 120 frames
per second, at a constant frame rate — much smaller than the equivalent MOV, and
constant frame rate is what stops audio drifting out of sync after an upload.

## What do you give up?

Instant. QuickTime is a keystroke and it is already installed; Prequel is an
application you have to get. For a ten-second clip to paste into a chat, opening
a recorder with an editor attached is more machinery than the job needs.

Both permissions are the same either way, incidentally. If your QuickTime
recordings have ever come out black, that is the Screen Recording permission,
not the app — macOS returns empty frames rather than an error when it has not
been granted, which reads as broken software rather than a missing checkbox.
