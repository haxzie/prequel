Most internal training video is made once, badly, by someone who was not hired
to make video. It shows the process, it is twenty minutes long, and it is
recorded at one distance with a cursor skidding around the screen.

It still beats writing the process down, which is why people keep making them.
The gap is that the tooling assumes you will edit afterwards, and nobody
editing an internal training video has ever had the afternoon for it.

## Do the process, get the video

Start recording, do the task the way you would do it anyway, stop. Prequel
notes where you click and where you type as it captures, and the editor opens
on the take with those moments already turned into zooms — pushed in on the
field being filled, pulled back when you move on.

A process that spans several applications works the same way. Record the whole
screen and cursor zooms follow the pointer between them, so moving from a
browser to a spreadsheet does not lose the person watching.

## The details that make it look careless

Two things give an internal recording away. The cursor is the first: at export
size it is either invisible or it sits parked over the thing being explained.
Prequel offers four pointer styles, resizes the cursor so it survives a zoom,
and takes it out of frame after a few seconds of stillness.

The second is audio. Something always arrives at full volume in the middle of
a take — a notification, a call, a video someone left playing. Microphone and
system audio are recorded as separate tracks with separate gains, so that is a
slider afterwards rather than a reason to record the whole thing again.

## Keeping it current

The take is a timeline. When step four changes, trim step four out and record a
replacement. The rest of the video does not have to be touched, and the new
section does not have to match the old one's framing to sit next to it.
