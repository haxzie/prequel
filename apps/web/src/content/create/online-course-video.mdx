A lesson is longer than anything else you will record on a screen, and length
is what exposes a flat recording. Forty minutes at one distance from the viewer
is genuinely tiring to watch, in a way that four minutes is not.

Course platforms are full of advice about this. It usually amounts to: edit
more. Add zooms, cut the pauses, vary the shot. All correct, and all of it
priced in hours per finished minute.

## The pass is already made

Prequel records where you click and type while it captures, and clusters those
moments into zooms before the editor opens. What you get back is a lesson that
already changes distance with the material — in on the thing being
demonstrated, out when you are talking over it.

Every zoom is an ordinary timeline slice, so disagreeing with one is a drag
rather than a rebuild. Level, speed, tilt and yaw are per zoom, with blur
falling away progressively from the focus, so a slow shallow push for a
walkthrough and a faster tighter one for a highlight are the same control set
at different values.

## A course has to look like one course

Lesson one and lesson eleven are recorded months apart, and it shows.

Because none of the look is decided while you record, it does not have to. The
camera shape, its size, its corner, the background, the padding, the radius,
the border and the shadow are all settings applied to a finished take. A lesson
recorded today can be given exactly the treatment you gave the first one.

## Files you can actually host

Export runs through your Mac's own media engine — hardware H.264 or HEVC,
composited in Metal, at a constant frame rate. A forty-minute lesson comes out
as one MP4 at a size a platform will accept, rather than the enormous MOV a
plain screen recording leaves behind.
