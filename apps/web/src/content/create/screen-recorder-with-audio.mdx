This is the oldest complaint about screen recording on a Mac. macOS exposes no
route to system audio through QuickTime, so the standard advice is to install a
virtual audio driver like BlackHole, route your output through it, record, and
then remember to route it back.

It works. It is also a kernel-adjacent audio driver installed to solve a
recording problem, and it silently breaks your speakers the first time you
forget the last step.

## Both, directly, as separate tracks

Prequel captures system audio and your microphone alongside the screen, with no
virtual device involved and nothing to switch back afterwards.

They are separate tracks with separate gains. That distinction is worth more
than it sounds. A notification landing at full volume over a sentence is a
slider afterwards instead of a reason to record the whole thing again, and a
demo where the app's own sound matters can have that sound sitting under your
narration at whatever balance you want rather than whatever balance the room
happened to produce.

The webcam is a fourth track, captured at the same time and never burned into
the screen recording.

## Why your screen recording is black

If you have arrived here from a recording that came out black: that is almost
always the Screen Recording permission. macOS hands back empty frames rather
than an error when it has not been granted, which reads as a broken app rather
than a missing checkbox. Grant it under Privacy and Security in System
Settings, then start the recording again.

## What you get out

One MP4, H.264 or HEVC, at up to 4K and up to 120 frames per second, at a
constant frame rate — which is what keeps audio in sync after an upload.

Recording, editing and export all happen locally, on your Mac's own media
engine. Nothing is uploaded to produce the file.
