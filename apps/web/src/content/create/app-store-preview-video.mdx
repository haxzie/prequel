An app preview is thirty seconds long, autoplays without sound, and sits next
to preview videos from teams with a motion designer. It is the least forgiving
video format there is.

It also cannot be a plain screen recording. At the size it plays back, a
faithful capture of a whole interface is unreadable, and the store's frame
requirements mean a landscape recording usually arrives cropped.

## Push in, because nothing else will

Prequel records where you click and type while it captures, and turns those
moments into zooms before the editor opens. On a preview that is the entire
game: thirty seconds is enough for three or four moments, each one filling the
frame while it happens.

Region zooms are worth knowing about here. They hold still on an area you draw,
which is what you want when the point is a screen the viewer has to read rather
than a pointer moving. Level and speed are per zoom, so a preview can move
quickly without feeling frantic.

## The frame the store asks for

Frame presets cover 16:9, 4K, vertical 9:16, square 1:1 and portrait 4:5.
Because framing is stored as proportions of the shorter edge rather than in
pixels, moving a take between them re-frames it instead of cropping it — the
padding stays proportional and anything you positioned stays where you put it.

## Details that read as production

The cursor leaves frame after a few seconds of stillness, which is the
difference between a preview that looks authored and one that looks like
somebody's desktop. It also has four styles and a size that survives a zoom.

Behind the recording sits a background — your own wallpaper, one of seven
bundled presets, a gradient or a solid — with padding, corner radius, border
and shadow on top.

Export is hardware H.264 or HEVC at up to 4K, made on your own machine.
