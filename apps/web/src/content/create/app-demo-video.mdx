An app demo is judged against the videos on the store listing next to yours,
and those were made by people with a motion designer. That is an unfair
comparison and it is the one you get.

What actually separates the two is not budget. It is that a produced video
changes distance — it pushes in on the moment that matters and pulls back when
it does not — and a screen recording never does.

## Record the app, not the screenshot

Pick a display, a window or a dragged region and start. A window capture is
usually right here — it keeps the rest of the desktop out of frame without you
having to tidy it first, and a dragged region works when the interesting part
is a panel rather than the whole app.

While it captures, Prequel is noting where you click and type. When you stop,
the editor opens on the take with those moments already turned into zooms, the
camera framed, and a background applied behind the recording.

## Shape it for wherever it is going

Store listings and social posts want different frames, and a landscape
recording that gets cropped to vertical usually loses half of what it was
showing.

Prequel stores every geometry setting as a fraction of the frame's shorter
edge rather than in pixels. Switch a take from 16:9 to 9:16 and the camera stays
a quarter of the way across the frame instead of sliding off it, the padding
stays proportional, and the look survives the change. Presets cover 16:9, 4K,
vertical 9:16, square, 4:5 and the named social sizes.

## What it costs you

One recording and a few minutes of disagreeing with the automatic pass.
Everything happens on your Mac — capture, editing and export all run on its own
media engine, so there is no upload, no queue and no cloud render between you
and the file.
