Onboarding videos have a completion problem. The metric that matters is not
whether someone pressed play, it is whether they were still watching at the
step that makes the product click — and most of them are not.

Part of that is length. A larger part is that a flat screen recording gives the
viewer no signal about what to look at. Everything on screen is the same size
and the same distance away, for eleven minutes.

## Give the eye somewhere to go

Prequel watches where you click and type while it records, and turns those
moments into zooms before you ever open the editor. The effect is that the
video moves with the task: in on the field being filled, out when you move to
the next part of the screen.

That is the thing viewers read as production value, and it is the thing nobody
has time to do by hand on a video that will need re-recording next quarter.

## Because it will need re-recording

Onboarding videos go stale faster than any other kind. The interface changes
and suddenly minute four is showing a screen that no longer exists.

The take is a timeline of slices, so the fix is a trim and a replacement
recording rather than a fresh start. Layout, background and audio can change
mid-take, so the ninety seconds you record today do not have to match the
framing choices you made last spring for the rest of it to keep working.

## One take, several videos

The same recording is usually wanted at several lengths — the full walkthrough
for the help centre, ninety seconds for the in-app tour, thirty for an email.

Trim it down and export again at a different frame preset. Framing is stored in
proportions rather than pixels, so the short vertical cut keeps the camera and
the padding where you put them instead of cropping them out of frame.
