On a launch post the video is the pitch. People scroll, the video autoplays,
and roughly six seconds later they have decided whether to read the words. A
raw screen capture spends those six seconds showing a desktop.

The good launch videos are not expensive so much as edited. They cut early,
they push in on the moment the product does its trick, and they never linger.

## Get to the trick

Record the walkthrough once. Prequel notes your clicks and keystrokes as it
captures and opens the editor with the zooms already placed on them, so the
first thing you see is a version that already moves.

From there it is subtraction. The timeline has a waveform under every clip and
a playhead that scrubs, so cutting a four-minute take down to sixty seconds is
a handful of blade cuts rather than an evening. Zoom level and speed are set per
zoom, which is how a fast cut still reads as deliberate.

## One take, every placement

A launch needs the same video in several shapes — landscape for the post,
vertical for social, maybe square.

Switch the frame preset and export again. Framing is stored as a fraction of
the frame's shorter edge, so the vertical cut keeps the camera and the padding
where you put them rather than cropping them out. The look holds across all of
them without a second recording.

## No render queue on launch day

Everything happens on your Mac. Capture, compositing and export all run on its
own media engine — hardware H.264 or HEVC, composited in Metal — so there is no
upload, no queue and nothing between you and the file at the moment you are
least able to wait for it.

What comes out is one MP4 at up to 4K, with no watermark on it.
