The live demo is expensive. It costs a calendar slot on both sides, it happens
at the speed of whoever is talking, and half of it is the same six minutes you
gave the last three prospects.

A recorded demo is not a replacement for the call that closes. It is a
replacement for the call that qualifies — the one where someone wants to see
whether the product does the thing before they commit an hour to you.

## It has to hold up without you

A live demo survives a rough recording because you are there to narrate around
it. A sent one does not. If the text is too small to read, nobody asks you to
zoom in; they close the tab.

Prequel places the zooms while you record, from where you click and type, so
the part you are talking about is the part filling the frame. Capture runs at
your display's native resolution, which means a push lands on real detail
rather than an upscale of it.

## Record long, send short

Record the full walkthrough once. What you send is a trim of it.

Cuts are slices on a real timeline, with a waveform under every clip and a
playhead that scrubs, so pulling a two-minute version out of a nine-minute take
is a few blade cuts. Layout, background and audio can all change mid-take,
which means the short version does not have to inherit the framing decisions
the long one made.

## Your face is optional, and reversible

The webcam is written as its own track and is never burned into the screen
recording. A take recorded with the camera on can ship without it, or with it in
a different corner, a different shape or a different size. That decision is not
one you have to get right before you press record.

Export is one MP4 in H.264 or HEVC, at up to 4K, made entirely on your own
machine.
