Nearly every changelog entry would be better as fifteen seconds of the feature
working. Almost none of them are, because the video costs more than the
paragraph and the paragraph ships today.

The cost is not the recording. It is everything after it: importing, placing
zooms, framing a camera, trimming, exporting, and doing all of it again next
release.

## Short enough to be worth it

Prequel removes the part in the middle. Record the feature once; the editor
opens on the take with the zooms already placed from where you clicked and
typed, the camera framed, a background applied and the cursor cleaned up.

For a fifteen-second clip that is very close to done. Trim the top and tail on
the timeline and export.

## Consistency without a template

The thing that makes a run of changelog clips look like a series is that they
share a treatment. Normally that means a project file you remember to reuse.

Here the treatment is settings on the take — background, padding, corner
radius, border, shadow, the camera's shape and size and corner. They are not
decisions you re-make in an editor each time, so a clip from this release
matches one from three releases ago without any effort spent on matching it.

## Wherever the changelog goes

The same clip is usually wanted in the release notes, in a post and sometimes
vertically for social. Frame presets cover 16:9, 4K, 9:16, square, 4:5 and the
named social sizes, and because framing is stored in proportions rather than
pixels the vertical export re-frames the take instead of cropping it.

Export is one MP4 at up to 4K, rendered on your Mac. No upload, no queue, and
nothing to wait for on the day you are shipping.
