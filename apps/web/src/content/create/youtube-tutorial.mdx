Screen-recorded YouTube content lives or dies on retention, and retention on a
tutorial is mostly a function of whether the viewer can see what you are doing.
A 1440p recording of a 15-inch display, watched on a phone, is not a video —
it is a wide shot of some text.

## Record at native resolution, push in afterwards

Capture runs at your display's own resolution, so there is headroom to zoom
into. A Retina Mac recording a 3024 by 1898 screen and exporting at 1080p has
roughly three times the pixels it needs, which means a 2x push lands on real
detail rather than an upscale of it.

Prequel places those pushes for you. It notes where you click and type during
the take, and the editor opens with the zooms already clustered onto those
moments. What arrives is closer to an edit than to footage.

## The parts you would otherwise do in post

The webcam is captured as its own track and never burned into the screen
recording, so its shape, size, corner and whether it is mirrored are all still
open when you stop. It can also come and go mid-video, because layout is
allowed to change across the take.

Backgrounds, padding, corner radius, border and shadow are settings rather than
a compositing job. The cursor gets four styles, a size that survives a zoom, and
an exit from frame when it has been still for a few seconds.

Dead air comes out on a real timeline, with a waveform under every clip.

## Export

One MP4, H.264 or HEVC, up to 4K and up to 120 frames per second, at a constant
frame rate — which matters, because variable frame rate is what makes an
upload's audio drift out of sync.

There is no watermark, no export limit and no upload step. Recording, editing
and rendering all happen on your Mac.
