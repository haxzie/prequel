A tutorial is a sequence of small targets. Click this, type that, notice the
thing that changed in the corner. Every one of those is a moment where the
viewer needs to be looking at a specific part of a screen that is mostly not
that part.

Screen recorders do not help with this. They capture the whole display
faithfully and hand you the problem.

## Zooms that land where you were working

Prequel records your clicks and keystrokes alongside the picture. When you stop,
the editor opens with those moments already grouped into zooms, so the tutorial
pushes in on each target as you reach it and pulls back out between them.

You are editing a first draft rather than raw footage. Every generated zoom is
an ordinary slice — move it, retime it, delete it, or point it somewhere else
entirely.

## Two kinds of push, for two kinds of step

A cursor zoom follows the pointer, which is right for a step where you are
moving across the interface.

A region zoom holds still on an area you draw, which is right for anything the
viewer has to read: a form, a config file, a diff, an error message. A zoom that
follows the cursor while someone is trying to read a paragraph is actively
worse than staying wide.

There is a third target — whatever you are typing into — for the steps where
the interesting thing is the text appearing rather than the pointer.

## The cursor itself

At tutorial scale the pointer matters more than it sounds. Prequel draws it in
one of four styles, resized so it is still visible after a 2x push, smoothed so
it stops skidding, and removed from frame after a few seconds of stillness
instead of sitting on top of the thing you just explained.

Export is one MP4, up to 4K and up to 120 frames per second, with no watermark
and no upload.
