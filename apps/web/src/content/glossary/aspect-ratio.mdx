Aspect ratio is the shape of the frame: its width against its height, written
as two numbers. 16:9 is a landscape rectangle, the shape of a display, a
television and a YouTube player. 9:16 is the same rectangle stood on end, the
shape of a phone held upright and of Shorts, Reels and TikTok. 1:1 is a square
and 4:5 is a slightly tall portrait, both feed shapes.

## Where the mismatch comes from

A screen is landscape, so a screen recording is landscape. The places it is
posted are often not. A 16:9 recording in a 9:16 feed is a thin strip across
the middle of the phone with empty space above and below, and the text in it is
too small to read.

Stretching it to fit is worse. Everything in the frame is distorted, and the
viewer notices before they notice anything else.

## The way it is done

Keep the screen at its own proportions and change the frame around it. In a
vertical frame the recording sits in the middle at full width, a background
fills the space above and below, and that space is where the camera goes,
larger than it would be in a corner. The recording is untouched; the
composition is new.

In Prequel that is a preset in the [frame bar](/docs/frame-and-presets):
Vertical 9:16, YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels, Square and Portrait,
each at the pixel size the platform wants. The layout follows the frame,
because every size in it is stored as a share of the frame rather than in
pixels, so the camera at a third of the height stays a third of the height when
the frame turns.

## Which to use

16:9 for a website, an email, a docs page and YouTube. 9:16 for Shorts, Reels
and TikTok. 1:1 or 4:5 for a LinkedIn or Instagram feed post, where a taller
frame takes more of the screen as it scrolls past.
