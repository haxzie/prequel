System audio is whatever the Mac is playing: a video in a browser tab, a
notification, the app being demonstrated making a sound, a call. It is
distinct from the microphone, which hears the room, and a screen recording
can carry either, both or neither.

## Why it is awkward on a Mac

For most of macOS's history there was no way for an app to hear what the Mac
was playing. QuickTime Player and the Cmd+Shift+5 toolbar still cannot: they
record the screen and a microphone, and the sound from the speakers is not on
offer. The workaround was a virtual audio device such as BlackHole, routed
through Audio MIDI Setup so the recorder could listen to it as though it were
a microphone. It works, and it is fiddly, and it is how most guides on the
subject still read.

Since macOS 13 the screen capture API can hand an app the system's audio
alongside the frames, and a recorder built on it can record the Mac's sound
with nothing to install. Not every recorder asks for it. The post on
[recording a Mac with internal audio](/blog/record-mac-screen-with-internal-audio)
goes through which do.

## Keep it on its own track

The useful version of system audio is a separate track from the microphone.
Mixed together at record time, a notification chime over your sentence is in
the recording for good. Kept apart, it can be turned down or dropped
afterwards while your voice stays. Prequel records the two separately, and the
[Audio panel](/docs/audio) has an include switch and a level for each.
