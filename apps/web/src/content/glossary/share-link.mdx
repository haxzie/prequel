A share link is a URL that plays a recording in the browser. The recording is
uploaded once; the link is sent as many times as needed; the viewer clicks and
watches, with no file to download and nothing to install. Loom made the
pattern familiar and most recorders now offer one.

## Why not just send the file

A three-minute screen recording at 1080p is a couple of hundred megabytes. In a
chat that is a download the viewer waits for, on a phone it is a download they
skip, and in an email it does not send at all. A link opens at once and plays as
it loads.

A link can also be taken back. A file that has been sent has been sent; a
recording behind a link can be deleted, and every copy of the link stops
working at the same moment.

## What travels with it

The video, and whatever the recorder knows about it. A share page from a
recorder that transcribed the take can show the transcript beside the player,
offer the words as subtitles, and draw chapters from what was said, so a viewer
who wants the second half can click to it. A bare video file has none of that.

## The trade

A link means an upload. The recording leaves the Mac and sits on somebody's
server, which is fine for a walkthrough and a decision for an unreleased
product. The sensible arrangement is a recorder that asks: the file lands on
the Mac first, and the upload is a button you press or do not.

Prequel's [Share Link](/docs/share-links) is that button, in the export
dialog. The link is `prequel.sh/v/` and a short code, the page carries the
captions and chapters when the take was transcribed, and the shared library at
`prequel.sh/app` is where a link is copied again or deleted.
