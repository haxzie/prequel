Zoom and pan is the camera move a screen recording borrows from film: push in
on part of the frame so the viewer can see it, move with the action, and pull
back out when it is over. On a screen the camera is virtual, so the move is
made in the edit by scaling and shifting the recorded frame.

## Why screen recordings need it

A full display is big and a player is small. A 2880 by 1800 recording played
in a 700 pixel wide box shows every menu item at a few pixels tall, and the
viewer is left squinting at where the pointer went. Zooming to the part of the
screen being used shows it at a size they can read. Pulling back out afterwards
puts it in context, so they know where on the screen it was.

The effect also sets the pace. A zoom on each click gives a recording a rhythm
that a static capture does not have, which is most of what people mean when
they say a recording looks polished.

## Placing them

By hand, the work is to find each click in the timeline, add a zoom, set where
it points and how far in it goes, and set how long it holds. Ten minutes of
recording is a few dozen of those.

Prequel places them for you. It records where you click and type while it
captures, and when the editor opens the zooms are already on the timeline: one
for each place you worked, pushed in on it, pulled back when you moved on.
Each can be dragged, resized or deleted, and more can be drawn where you want
them. The [zooms](/docs/zooms) page has the controls, including a zoom that
follows the pointer and a tilt that gives the push some depth.
