macOS does not let an app see the screen until you say so. Screen Recording is
the grant that says so, listed under Privacy and Security in System Settings,
and an app without it is handed the desktop wallpaper with every window
removed. The app still records, the file still saves, and it is only on
playback that the screen turns out to be empty.

## What it covers

Any pixel that belongs to another app or to the system. An app can always
record its own windows; the moment it captures a browser, a menu bar or the
desktop, it needs the grant. On macOS 15 and later the same switch covers
[system audio](/glossary/system-audio), and the pane is named Screen &
System Audio Recording to say so.

## How it is granted

The first time an app asks, macOS shows a dialog with a button that opens the
right pane in System Settings. Turn the app on there, and then quit and reopen
the app: the grant is read when the app starts, and one that asked before it
was granted has to ask again. That restart is the step most guides leave out
and the reason "I allowed it and it still records nothing" is so common.

Since macOS 15, the system also re-confirms the grant every so often with a
dialog that says the app can access this computer's screen and audio. It is a
reminder that the grant exists; clicking Continue to Allow keeps things as
they were.

## The other three

A recorder usually wants more than one grant. The microphone and the camera
each have their own, asked for only when the device is turned on. Accessibility
is the odd one: it lets an app see where you click and type, which is nothing
to do with the picture and everything to do with placing
[zooms](/glossary/zoom-and-pan) on the clicks afterwards. Prequel's
[permissions](/docs/permissions) page lists all four and what each one is for.
