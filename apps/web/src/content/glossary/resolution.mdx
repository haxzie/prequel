Resolution is the size of the picture in pixels, width by height. The names are
shorthand for the height: 1080p is 1920 by 1080, 1440p is 2560 by 1440, and 4K
is 3840 by 2160, which is four times as many pixels as 1080p rather than four
times the height. 720p, 1280 by 720, is the floor for anything with text in it.

## Where a screen recording's resolution comes from

It starts as the display's. A Retina MacBook reports a resolution like 1440 by
900 in System Settings, but the panel behind it has twice as many pixels in
each direction, 2880 by 1800, and a recorder that captures every pixel records
at that size. That is what you want: text drawn at full pixel density stays
sharp, and it can be scaled down later. A recorder that captures at the
reported size records a quarter of the detail and there is no getting it back.
The [Retina and HiDPI](/glossary/retina-hidpi) entry has the rest.

Recording one window rather than the whole display gives a recording the size
of that window, which is often the better choice: a browser at 1400 pixels
wide fills a 16:9 frame better than a whole desktop with a dock and a menu bar.

## What to export at

Full size if the file is going to YouTube or anywhere that re-encodes uploads,
because a larger upload is put in a higher [bitrate](/glossary/bitrate) tier
and comes out cleaner at every size it is served at. 1080p if it is going to a
person, a chat or an email, where a quarter of the size and the same legibility
is the better trade. Below 1080p only for a GIF, where every frame is stored in
full and the size runs away quickly.

Prequel [exports](/docs/export) at the frame's own size, or scales the shorter
edge to 1080, 720 or 480.
