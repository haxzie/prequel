Picture-in-picture is a second video drawn as a small window over the first.
In a screen recording it means the camera: the presenter's face in a corner of
the frame while the screen fills the rest. Webcam overlay, camera bubble and
facecam are the same thing under other names.

## What it is for

A face makes a recording easier to watch. The viewer can see who is talking,
read the emphasis, and stay with a three-minute walkthrough they would have
skimmed as a silent screen. It is also the difference between a demo and a
screencast in the viewer's mind: one is a recording, the other is a person
showing them something.

## Where it goes

A corner, over the part of the screen that matters least. Which corner depends
on the app: a sidebar on the left means the camera goes bottom right; a
toolbar along the bottom means it goes up. The mistake is putting it where the
work is, so the presenter's head covers the button being clicked.

Shape and size are taste. A circle reads as informal and takes less of the
frame; a rounded rectangle shows more of the room. A fifth of the frame's
height is a common size; larger than a third and it competes with the screen.

## Baked in or a layer

A recorder that composites the camera into the frames as it records has fixed
its position for good. One that records the camera as its own track can move,
resize, reshape or remove it in the edit, and switch to a camera-only layout
for the intro and back. Prequel does the second, and the
[camera](/docs/camera) and [layout](/docs/layout) pages cover the controls.
