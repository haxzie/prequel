A screencast is a screen recording with a voice over it, made to show somebody
else how something works. The word was coined in 2004, when recording a screen
still needed dedicated software, and it has kept its meaning while the tools
underneath it changed completely.

## Screencast or screen recording

A screen recording is what the recorder produces: frames of the display, with
or without audio. A screencast is the thing you send. In practice the two words
are used for each other, but screencast still carries the sense of narration
and of an audience. Nobody calls a silent clip of a bug a screencast.

## What a good one looks like

Short. A viewer decides in the first ten seconds whether to keep watching, and
a screencast that opens with the presenter finding the right window has lost
them. Two to three minutes is a comfortable ceiling; anything longer is better
as a few recordings a viewer can choose between.

Legible. The part of the screen being used should fill the frame, which is
what [zoom and pan](/glossary/zoom-and-pan) is for, and the text should be
sharp, which is what [capturing at full pixel size](/glossary/retina-hidpi)
is for.

Watchable with the sound off. A lot of screencasts are opened in a chat, on a
phone, or in an office, and the first decision to keep watching is made before
the sound is turned on. [Captions](/glossary/captions-and-subtitles) are what
carries the narration in those seconds.

## The shape

A face in the corner, a background behind the screen, the pointer visible and
the clicks emphasised. That shape is what Loom made familiar and Screen Studio
made cinematic, and it is the one Prequel produces from a plain recording
without the edit being done by hand.
