- A recording of a window opens with the window's own corners. The frame used to round every recording by the same amount, and a window's corners are rounder than that — so there was a sliver of background between the border and the picture at each corner. The recorder now measures the corners as it records, and a new project starts from them. Rounder or squarer is still yours to set; only the starting point changed, and a project you have already edited keeps what it has.
- Changing how a text arrives or leaves plays it back, the way changing a zoom does. Pick an entrance and it plays, with a beat of the text at rest after it; pick an exit and it plays with a beat before. Choosing a template plays the whole text when it is short.
- Adding a text leaves the playhead where you pressed. It used to hop forward a little the moment the text was made.
- A teleprompter, in the notch. Switch it on from the panel once a microphone is chosen, write what you want to say, and the words sit right under the camera while you record — and never in the recording. It follows your voice: the microphone opens when the take starts and closes when it stops, the word you are on lights up as you say it, it waits while you go off-script, and it catches up when you skip ahead or go back. Click a word, or scroll, to pick up from somewhere else; notes in [square brackets] are shown dimmed and never waited for. A Mac with no on-device speech model gets a steady scroll instead, and there are keys for moving by a sentence either way.
- The prompter can be hidden and shown mid-take from the recording pill without losing its place, and pinned to a particular display when more than one is connected. On a Mac without a notch it sits just under the menu bar instead.
