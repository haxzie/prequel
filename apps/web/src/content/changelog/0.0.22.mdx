- A recording of a window opens with the window's own corners. The frame used to round every recording by the same amount, and a window's corners are rounder than that — so there was a sliver of background between the border and the picture at each corner. The recorder now measures the corners as it records, and a new project starts from them. Rounder or squarer is still yours to set; only the starting point changed, and a project you have already edited keeps what it has.
- Changing how a text arrives or leaves plays it back, the way changing a zoom does. Pick an entrance and it plays, with a beat of the text at rest after it; pick an exit and it plays with a beat before. Choosing a template plays the whole text when it is short.
- Adding a text leaves the playhead where you pressed. It used to hop forward a little the moment the text was made.
- A teleprompter, in the notch. Switch it on from the panel once a microphone is chosen, write what you want to say, and the words sit right under the camera while you record — and never in the recording. It follows your voice: the microphone opens when the take starts and closes when it stops, the word you are on lights up as you say it, it waits while you go off-script, and it catches up when you skip ahead or go back. Click a word, or scroll, to pick up from somewhere else; notes in [square brackets] are shown dimmed and never waited for. A Mac with no on-device speech model gets a steady scroll instead, and there are keys for moving by a sentence either way.
- The prompter can be hidden and shown mid-take from the recording pill without losing its place, and pinned to a particular display when more than one is connected. On a Mac without a notch it sits just under the menu bar instead.
- Five new pointers with a name on them. Under Cursor, the last row of styles is a coloured pointer with a tag beside it, the way a collaborator's cursor is drawn in a shared canvas; pick one and type a name. Blue, purple, pink, orange and green.
- Typing and clicks can be heard. Under Sounds in the Audio panel, pick a keyboard — Tactile, Linear, Clicky or Thock, or the iPhone's own soft tock, whose space bar, Delete and Return each have their own sound — and a mouse, and the export carries keystrokes and clicks that land on the ones in the picture. The sounds are made by Prequel, not recorded: no microphone is involved, and on the mechanical keyboards no two presses sound quite the same. New projects start with soft clicks on and the keyboard off, ready to choose; a project you have already edited stays as it was. To make this possible a recording now notes the moment of each key press and whether it was a letter, the space bar, Return, Delete or a modifier — never which letter, and never in a password field. The switch is Note when keys are pressed, in Settings.
- Prequel asks for Input Monitoring. macOS gives an app your clicks under Accessibility but your keys only under this, and without it typing was never noticed at all — the pointer stayed over what you typed and there was nothing for the sounds to play. It is a new row in the permissions list; grant it and restart, as with the others.
- The prompter keeps up. It moves the moment you say the word it is waiting for, where it used to wait for the one after; and it listens with the microphone you chose in the panel rather than whichever one the Mac calls the default, which was the wrong one whenever a headset or a USB mic was picked.
- Changing the prompter's width or text size eases the island from one to the other. It used to slide the whole island in again.
- A pencil on the island opens the script, so it is one click from where it is read.
