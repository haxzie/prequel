- Recording with a Bluetooth headset as the microphone failed at the end of every take, and the recording was thrown away with it. A headset in hands-free mode records at a much lower sample rate than a built-in mic, and Prequel was asking the encoder for a quality that cannot be produced at that rate — so the sound was rejected, and the take went with it. The rate Prequel asks for now follows the microphone. Recordings made while this was happening were never written and cannot be recovered.
- A take could come out unplayable if what you were recording went away — the window you were capturing was closed, or a display was unplugged. Stopping the take failed halfway and left the video and audio files unfinished, so the recording sat in the library, opened, and never played. Stopping now finishes the files whatever the capture did. Recordings already in that state cannot be recovered.
- Split and delete now sit at the left end of the editor's playback row, ahead of the timecode, rather than over on the right. Undo still appears beside them the moment there is a step back to take.
- You can add more footage to a recording you have already made. Open it, press Add Recording in the row under the preview — next to Add Zoom and Add Text, or press R — and the recording panel comes back. Record, stop, and the new footage arrives as another clip at the end of the timeline. Your cuts, zooms, text and background stay exactly where they were.
- The editor stays open while you record the addition, and is kept out of both the picture and the list of things you can record — hover where it is and you get the window behind it. The source starts on whatever that recording was made of, and you can change it: a different window, a different screen, or a different size all sit in the frame correctly.
- The footage you add gets the same automatic zooms a new recording does, built from the clicks and typing in it. Zooms you placed yourself on the older footage are left where they are.
- The blue outline that appeared around a control in the recording panel after using one of its menus is gone. Moving through the panel with the keyboard still shows a ring, in the panel's own colours rather than the system's.
- A clip can wear a filter. Open Filters in the panel beside the preview and pick one: CRT, LCD, VHS, Film, Halftone, Fish eye, Pixelate, Colour, Glow, Chromatic aberration, or light through a window. It goes over the finished picture — background, recording, camera and captions together — so the whole frame looks like it was shot that way rather than like something was laid on top.
- Each one has its own controls underneath, in its own words. The CRT has a mask, a triad pitch and a phosphor colour, so an amber or green terminal is two clicks. Window light comes through blinds, window panes, a curtain or leaves, and you set where the light falls from. Halftone prints in one ink, two, or full CMYK.
- Filters are per clip, like everything else in that panel. Split a recording and the two halves can wear different looks, or none. Reset puts a clip back, Apply to all clips carries a look across the whole edit, and a saved look travels with your presets.
- The moving parts hold still unless you ask for them. A CRT's refresh bar, a tape's tracking and film's gate weave each have a switch, and all three start off — pleasant for five seconds is not the same as pleasant for five minutes.
- Clips can be dragged into a different order. Pick one up on the timeline and drop it between two others — a line shows where it will land — and the edit plays in the new order.
- Zooms stay with the footage they were placed over rather than with the moment in the edit. A clip you move carries its own zooms with it, and the clips either side keep theirs where they were.
- A text is its own length. It used to take the length of the clip beneath it, so splitting a recording under a title, or trimming the footage there, changed how long the title was on screen. What you set is what it stays.
- Dragging a text no longer snaps it to the clips underneath, and it keeps its entrance and exit — both were lost as soon as you picked one up or pulled an edge.
- Texts can be carried past one another along a row. A text that was third stayed third: dragging it towards the front stopped dead against its neighbour and sprang back. It goes wherever there is room on the row now, and it changes rows as you cross them rather than when you let go.
- Hovering a text row shows the outline of the text a click would add, the way the zoom row does — how long it will be and where it will sit, before you commit to it. A click adds it where you clicked.
- Adding a text leaves the playhead a beat inside it, with the words at rest and on screen. It used to sit on the one frame of a title's life where it is still completely transparent, so you added a title and nothing appeared to happen.
- A filter's controls open in a view of their own. Pick a look and the panel pushes across to its settings, with the look's swatch and name at the top and a way back — so the list of looks can grow without pushing the controls off the bottom.
- The fish eye reads as glass rather than as a stretch. The picture softens towards the rim and the colours part a little at the edge, both following the strength.
