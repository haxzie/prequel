- The automatic zoom rests on the field you are typing into, rather than a little to one side of it. A field wider than the shot — a title box across a form — is framed from the end the text starts at, so the label and the first words are on screen rather than the empty middle of the box.
- A field that merely has focus no longer counts as typing. A page that focuses its first box on arrival, or a chat box that keeps focus for the whole recording, used to pull every click around it into one long zoom held on the box until the end. The zoom now looks at a field only while you are typing into it, and follows the pointer the rest of the time.
- Where a stretch of the recording has both clicks and typing, the zoom follows the pointer. A click says where to look; a focused box only says where the caret is.
