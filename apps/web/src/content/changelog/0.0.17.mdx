- Exports use a fraction of the memory. The soundtrack was mixed whole before the first frame was drawn, which on a fifteen-minute edit was over a gigabyte. It is now mixed as it goes, and a long export holds steady at what a short one does.
- Exporting a trimmed clip no longer fails before it starts.
- A recording that macOS is not delivering frames for stops itself within two seconds and says so, rather than counting up for minutes and failing at the end. This is what a Screen Recording permission granted without restarting looks like.
- The tray menu offers Record Entire Screen, Record a Window and Record an Area, so you can choose the source without opening the panel first.
- A drawn area can be moved, and resized from its edges and corners, before you record it.
- Settings lists the four permissions and what each is for, and offers a restart where a grant needs one to take effect. The version sits at the foot of the pane, beside Check for Updates.
- The save dialog opens in Downloads rather than in the app's own recordings folder.
- Changing a caption font or look keeps the captions on screen while the new ones draw, rather than blanking them for a moment.
- The editor shows the recording once it has decoded, rather than a background with nothing on it for the first moment.
- The library opens as fast with a thousand recordings as with twenty. It loads a screen at a time and fetches more as you scroll.
- The timecode's digits roll as they change.
- The dock no longer flashes a scrollbar when a camera is turned on, and its menus no longer open empty the first time.
- When something goes wrong, the app tells us the shape of the failure, with every file path removed, so a bug that hits many people is seen without anyone having to write in.
