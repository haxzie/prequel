## The parts

![The editor: frame bar at the top, the composition in the middle, the Inspector on the right, the transport and timeline below](/docs/editor.webp)

From top to bottom:

- **Header.** A breadcrumb back to **Projects**, the recording's name, a wand that adds zooms automatically, a bin, and the green **Export** button.
- **Frame bar.** The output size and aspect. See [Frame and presets](/docs/frame-and-presets).
- **Composition.** A live preview of the finished video: the recording, the camera, the background, the pointer and the captions, drawn exactly as they will export. Click any of them to select it and open its panel. Drag to move, use the corner handles to resize.
- **Inspector.** A rail of icons down the right. Each opens a panel: Presets, Layout, Background, Recording, Camera, Audio, Cursor, Captions and Logo. Panels that do not apply to this recording are not offered; a take with no microphone has no Captions panel.
- **Transport.** The time, play, and the verbs: **Add Zoom**, undo, split, delete.
- **Timeline.** The clip row with a filmstrip and waveform, and the zoom row beneath it. See [Timeline and cuts](/docs/timeline-and-cuts) and [Zooms](/docs/zooms).

## Saving

There is no save button. Every change is written to the recording's own `project.json` a moment after you make it, and reopening the recording puts you back where you were. The video files themselves are never modified; an edit is a description of what to do with them.

**Command + Z** undoes the last change.

## The first pass

The first time a recording opens, Prequel looks at where you clicked and typed and adds a zoom for each. This needs the Accessibility permission to have been granted while recording. The wand in the header runs the pass again over the edit as it stands, adding zooms for any moment not already covered. It is disabled when the recording holds no clicks or typing to work from.

The first pass adds zooms only. It does not cut anything: trimming is yours to do on the timeline.

## Project defaults and per-clip overrides

Every panel edits the project's defaults, which apply to the whole recording. Select a clip on the timeline first and the same controls edit that clip alone. A dot beside a control shows it is overridden on the selected clip.

Two buttons in a panel's header manage this: **Reset to the project defaults** clears the overrides on this clip, and **Apply these settings to every clip** copies this clip's look to the rest.
