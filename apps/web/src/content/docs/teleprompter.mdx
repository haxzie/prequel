## What it is

![The teleprompter hung from the notch, a line into the script](/docs/teleprompter.webp)

The teleprompter puts your script in the notch, a few millimetres under the camera, so your eyes stay on the lens while you read. It follows your voice: the word you are on lights up as you say it, and the text moves on as you go.

It is on your screen and nowhere else. Prequel keeps its own windows out of the capture, so the island is never in the recording — not for a screen, a window or an area.

## Switching it on

![The panel with the teleprompter on and set to follow your voice](/docs/dock-teleprompter.webp)

The prompter has a switch on the panel, after the microphone. Turn it on and the island appears; the chevron beside it opens a menu with **Edit Script…**, how the words move, and the text size. The menu bar's **Teleprompter** item does the same, and **Edit Teleprompter Script…** opens the script.

Turning it on from the panel before you have written anything opens the script window straight away.

## Writing the script

![The script window: the text, the switch, how it moves, and the size](/docs/script-window.webp)

Type or paste what you want to say. There is no Save: the script is remembered as you type, and the island shows it as it is.

A few habits make it read better:

- **One thought per line.** A line break is where the step keys land, and where the island breaks the text.
- **Notes to yourself go in square brackets.** `[smile]` or `[pause here]` are drawn dim and in italics, and the prompter never waits for you to say them. Only square brackets count — `(pause)` and `<pause>` are ordinary words, and the prompter will wait for you to say "pause".
- **Numbers can be written either way.** "2026" is matched whether you say "twenty twenty-six" or "two thousand and twenty-six", and "3" matches "three".

The window closes itself when a take starts. It is an ordinary window, and it would be in the recording.

## How it follows you

Prequel listens on this Mac — nothing leaves it — and lines up the last few words it heard against the script to find where you are. That is what lets it cope with a reader who is not a machine:

- **Go off-script** and the highlight waits where you left off. Ramble as long as you like; the words in the footer pulse while it listens for you to come back.
- **Skip a line** and it catches up within a couple of words.
- **Go back a sentence** and it follows you back.
- **Click any word** to say "I am here", and it carries on from there. The scroll wheel does the same: scroll, and whichever word ends up on the reading line is where it picks up.

Your script's own words are handed to the recogniser to expect, so product names and unusual words are heard right more often than they would be in dictation.

The microphone opens when the take starts and closes when it stops. Before that the island shows your script and stays still — read it over, step through it with the keys — but nothing is listening.

## Three ways to move

Under **Moves** in the script window, or in the panel's menu:

- **Voice** follows what you say. The default.
- **Auto-scroll** moves at a steady pace, set in words a minute. It starts with the take, so the text does not run off during the countdown.
- **Manual** moves only on the keys, a click or the wheel.

A Mac with no on-device speech model for your language cannot follow your voice, and says so in the island's footer — it auto-scrolls instead. Add the language under **Keyboard › Dictation** in System Settings and it will listen next time.

## Keys

These work while the island is showing, whatever application is in front:

| Keys                     | Does              |
| ------------------------ | ----------------- |
| Control + Option + Down  | On a sentence.    |
| Control + Option + Up    | Back a sentence.  |
| Control + Option + Space | Pause and resume. |
| Control + Option + 0     | Back to the top.  |

They carry Control and Option because plain arrows, and Option with arrows, are how you move through text in the very editor you are likely to be recording.

## Where it sits

On a MacBook with a notch the island hangs from it, drawn as the notch grown wide. On a Mac without one it sits at the top of the built-in display, just under the menu bar — the camera is still up there. With the lid closed and an external display, it takes the top of the display the pointer is on.

**Text** and **Width** in the script window change the island's size. Four lines are always showing, with the one you are reading second from the top, so you can see what comes next.
