## What is a one-time purchase screen recorder?

A one-time purchase screen recorder is an app you pay for once and keep. The
licence does not renew, there is no monthly charge, and the app keeps working
whether or not the company does. Most of them let you record and edit for free
and charge for the export, so the price is the price of the finished file.

The detail to check is what "once" covers. Some licences include every future
update, some include a year of updates and then ask for a renewal, and some
cover the current major version only. Each tool below says which.

## 1. Prequel

![Prequel's website](/blog/tools/prequel.webp)

Prequel is $29, once. That buys the app with nothing held back: 4K export at
up to 120 fps, no watermark on any plan or during the trial, no limit on how
long a take can run, and rendering done on your own Mac with nothing uploaded.
There is a $9 a month plan too, and the app in both is the same app.

What the $29 is really buying is a video that is already edited when the
recording stops. Zooms are placed on every click and burst of typing, the
camera is framed, the background is applied and the cursor is cleaned up before
you see the timeline. Because the screen and camera are recorded as separate
sources, you can re-frame the camera after the take.

The code is on GitHub under FSL-1.1-ALv2, which becomes Apache 2.0 two years
after each release, so the app you paid for once is one you can also read.

**Price.** $29 once, or $9 a month. Seven-day trial, exports included.
**Requires.** An Apple Silicon Mac on macOS 14 or later.

## 2. Cap

![Cap's website](/blog/tools/cap.webp)

Cap is free for personal use with no time limit on local recordings, and a
commercial desktop licence is $29 a year or $58 once. The code is on GitHub,
it runs on Windows and Linux as well as macOS, and Studio Mode gives you a
real editor with zooms and backgrounds.

Read the licence before you record for work. The free tier is personal use
only, so a product demo or a client video needs the paid one, and the $58
lifetime licence is twice Prequel's. Cap exports 4K at 60 fps where Prequel
runs to 120.

**Price.** Free for personal use, then $29/yr or $58 once.

## 3. CleanShot X

![CleanShot X's website](/blog/tools/cleanshot-x.webp)

CleanShot X is the screenshot tool most Mac people already own, and it records
video too, with a Studio Mode that adds zooms with motion blur. CleanShot
Basic is $35 once with a year of updates, then an optional $19 a year to keep
them coming. CleanShot Pro is a subscription for the cloud side.

If you want scrolling capture, annotations and a recorder in one menu bar
icon, nothing else here does it. Its video is a feature of a screenshot app,
though: the zooms are placed by hand after the take, where Prequel's are on
the timeline before you open it.

**Price.** $35 once, updates for one year, then $19/yr if you want more.

## 4. CursorClip

![CursorClip's website](/blog/tools/cursorclip.webp)

CursorClip is a native Mac app that adds zooms and pans as you record, with a
teleprompter, layouts and saved looks in version 2. The lifetime licence is
$70, covers three Macs, and works offline after activation. There are $9 a
month and $29 a year plans as well.

Recording and editing are free; export is what the licence unlocks. That is the
same shape as most of this list, and it is where Prequel's trial is different:
seven days of the whole app, exports included, so the video you make while
deciding is a file you can publish.

**Price.** $70 once, or $9/mo or $29/yr.

## 5. Screen Charm

![Screen Charm's website](/blog/tools/screen-charm.webp)

Screen Charm is a one-person Mac recorder with automatic zooms, captions, blur
regions, a split vertical layout for Shorts and a MacBook device frame. It is
$99 once for up to three Macs with lifetime updates and a 14-day money-back
guarantee.

It is the dearest one-off on this page, three times Prequel's $29, and like
CursorClip it locks the export until you pay. Screen Charm's export runs to 4K
at 60 fps; Prequel's runs to 120.

**Price.** $99 once, three Macs.
**Requires.** An Apple Silicon Mac on macOS 14 or later.

## 6. ScreenFlow

![ScreenFlow's website](/blog/tools/screenflow.webp)

ScreenFlow is the old-school answer: a full timeline editor with a recorder
attached, $199 once for a single machine, with a stock media library sold
separately as a subscription. It runs on Intel and Apple Silicon Macs and has
been around long enough that every tutorial channel has used it.

It is also a manual editor. Every zoom, every cut and every camera position is
yours to place, which is the work Prequel has done before the editor opens,
for about a seventh of the money.

**Price.** $199 once, single machine.

## 7. FocuSee

![FocuSee's website](/blog/tools/focusee.webp)

FocuSee adds zooms, cursor animation and backgrounds automatically and runs
on Windows as well as macOS. The one-time licence is $199.99 for up to five
computers, and the yearly plans start at $49.99.

Read the small print on "lifetime": it is lifetime access to version 2, and
moving to version 3 is a paid upgrade. Export tops out at 4K 60 fps against
Prequel's 4K 120, and Prequel's $29 is the price of the app, not of one
version of it.

**Price.** $199.99 once for version 2, or $49.99/yr and up.

## 8. OBS Studio

![OBS Studio's website](/blog/tools/obs.webp)

Free, open source, no watermark and no time limit. OBS records anything you
can point it at, streams live, and runs on an Intel Mac. If no subscription
and no money at all is the requirement, this is the answer.

What it does not do is edit. There is no timeline, so the zooms, the cuts and
the framing happen in another app afterwards, and that is the part Prequel
hands you finished.

**Price.** Free.

[The open source Mac recorders, compared →](/blog/open-source-screen-recorders-mac)

## 9. QuickTime Player

![QuickTime Player's support page](/blog/tools/quicktime.webp)

QuickTime is already on your Mac and makes a clean MP4 in four clicks. For a
quick clip that only has to be legible, use it and stop reading.

It records the screen or the camera, not both together, and it cannot hear
the Mac's own audio without a virtual driver. Prequel records screen, camera,
microphone and system audio as separate tracks in one take.

**Price.** Free with macOS.
