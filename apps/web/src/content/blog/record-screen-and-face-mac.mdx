## How do you record your screen and your face at the same time?

Not with anything Apple ships. QuickTime Player records a screen capture or a
camera movie, never both into one file, and the ⇧⌘5 toolbar records a display
or a region with no camera option at all.

So every route is a third-party one, and they differ in one thing that matters
more than any feature: whether your face is burned into the picture while you
record, or kept as its own track and placed afterwards.

That difference is the whole of why people re-record. You finish a six-minute
walkthrough, watch it back, and your head is sitting over the button you spend
the last minute pressing. If the camera was composited at capture there is
nothing to do about it. If it was a separate track, you drag it.

This is what macOS gives you, the three routes people take, and how to get your
face on a screen recording without deciding where it goes before you start.

## What macOS gives you

QuickTime Player does both, one at a time. **File**, then **New Screen
Recording** captures the screen and a microphone. **File**, then **New Movie
Recording** captures the camera. Run both and you have two files and no way to
put one on the other.

The ⇧⌘5 toolbar is the same capture with a toolbar on it. Display, region,
microphone. No camera.

## The three routes people take

**Build a scene in OBS.** Add a display capture, add a video capture device,
drag the camera where you want it, crop it to a circle with a mask. It is free
and it works and it composites at capture, so the position you set before you
start is the position you have forever. Budget half an hour the first time.

**Use a recorder that puts a bubble in the corner.** Loom and most browser
recorders do this. One click, your face is in the bottom left, and the file is
finished when you stop. Fast, and the bubble is where the bubble is.

**Record the camera separately and composite it yourself.** Two files into
Final Cut or DaVinci Resolve, line up the audio, mask the camera, keyframe it
out of the way when it covers something. Total control and an afternoon.

**Record the camera as its own track and frame it in the editor.** The capture
keeps the camera in a separate file, and where it sits, how big it is and what
shape it is are decisions you make with the finished take in front of you.

That last one is what Prequel does.

## How to record your screen and your face in Prequel

[Prequel](/) records the screen, the camera, the microphone and your Mac's own
sound as four separate tracks, then opens an editor on the take with the camera
already framed.

### 1. Turn the camera on in the dock

The dock has a camera control: the device's icon, a status dot and a chevron.
Press it and pick your camera from the menu of everything the Mac can see. A
green dot means it is on and available, grey means off, and red means it is
switched on but something else is holding it.

Prequel remembers the device by name, so the same camera is chosen after a
restart even when macOS hands it a new identifier.

### 2. Check your framing in the bubble, then forget it

A round preview appears on screen so you can see yourself before you record.
Drag it anywhere; it stays where you put it.

The bubble is not in the recording. It is there so you can check you are in
frame and lit, and the camera is captured as its own track at full size no
matter where the bubble sat. Nothing you do to it affects the file.

### 3. Record, and let the first pass frame you

Press ⇧⌘5. When you stop, the editor opens with the zooms already placed where
you clicked and typed, your camera framed over the screen, a background behind
both and the audio balanced.

The default layout is **Padded screen, camera over**: the screen inset with
padding around it, your camera over the corner. Seven more are a click away,
including the camera standing over one end, the two side by side, and a split
down the middle.

### 4. Change the shape and the size

Open **Camera** in the inspector down the right-hand side.

![The Camera panel: shape, roundness, size, zoom, a position map and mirror](/docs/inspector-camera.webp)

**Shape** is Circle, Squircle, Rounded, Wide or Portrait, plus **Remove
background**, which cuts you out of your surroundings and leaves you over the
composition's background. It works from a person matte recorded alongside the
camera, so it is offered on takes made since the matte existed.

**Size** is the camera's height as a share of the frame. **Zoom** enlarges the
picture inside its shape, which crops the edges and is how you sit closer
without moving your chair. **Roundness** rounds the corners of the rectangular
shapes.

### 5. Move it off the thing it is covering

The map at the bottom of the panel puts the camera at one of nine positions.
Dragging it in the composition gives you any position at all, and holding
Option while you drag pans the picture inside its box.

**Mirror** flips the camera so it matches what you see in a mirror. It is on by
default, because that is how most people expect to look.

### 6. Make it shrink when the screen zooms

Turn on **Shrink on zoom** and set **Size while zoomed**. Your camera gets
smaller for the length of every zoom, so it covers less of the thing you zoomed
in to look at.

This is the setting worth turning on before anything else. A camera that is the
right size over a full screen is too big over a zoomed one, and this is the one
control that fixes it everywhere at once.

### 7. Fix the room you recorded in

Open **Effects** in the Camera panel. Six colour looks, applied to the camera
alone: Warm for a cold room or a window behind you, Cool for a warm bulb
overhead, Film for lifted blacks and warm highlights.

Hover a look to see it on your own face before you choose it, and set
**Amount** for how much of it you want. The screen beside you is untouched,
which is right: it is lit by a display, not by the light you were sitting in.

**Edge** puts a glass rim on the camera, with **Depth** for how thick it is and
**Fringing** for how much colour it splits at the edge.

## Four things that make a talking head work over a screen

**Put yourself where your interface is not.** The bottom right corner is the
default everywhere and is also where most apps put their primary button. Look
at your own screen before you record and pick the empty corner.

**Smaller than you think.** The screen is the content. Your face is there so the
viewer knows a person is talking, and a quarter of the frame height is plenty.

**Look at the camera at the start and the end.** The middle is you working, and
looking at your own screen while you work is correct. The first and last
sentence are addressed to somebody.

**Record a microphone.** The camera is the picture; the microphone is the
reason anybody stays. It also gets you the transcript, which is how the
captions and the fastest route to a cut both work.
