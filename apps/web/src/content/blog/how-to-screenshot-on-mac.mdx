## How do you take a screenshot on a Mac?

Shift + Command + 3 saves a picture of every display. Shift + Command + 4 gives
you a crosshair to drag a region out with. Both write a PNG to your Desktop.

Press Space after ⇧⌘4 and the crosshair becomes a camera that captures the
window under it with its shadow, which is the one most people want and the one
nobody is taught.

So the capture is solved. What is not solved is everything after it: the file
lands on your Desktop called `Screenshot 2026-11-25 at 14.29.11.png`, sized to
whatever your display happens to be, with a customer's email address visible in
the corner and no way to point at the thing you were trying to show.

This is every chord macOS binds, what Markup can and cannot do to the result,
and how to get a screenshot you can put in a changelog or a support reply
without opening a design tool.

## The four chords

| Keys                | Does                                                      |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shift + Command + 3 | Saves a picture of every display, straight away.          |
| Shift + Command + 4 | Drag out a region. Press Space first to capture a window. |
| Shift + Command + 5 | Opens the Screenshot toolbar, with recording and Options. |
| Shift + Command + 6 | The Touch Bar, on a Mac that has one.                     |

Hold **Control** with any of them and the picture goes on the clipboard and no
file is written. That is the fastest path into Slack: ⌃⇧⌘4, drag, ⌘V.

While the crosshair is up, **Space** switches to window capture, holding
**Option** while you drag resizes from the centre, and **Esc** cancels.

## Where they go, and how to change it

The Desktop, by default, which is how a Desktop ends up with ninety of them.

Press ⇧⌘5 and open **Options**. The top of that menu sets where screenshots are
saved: Desktop, Documents, Clipboard, Mail, Messages, Preview, or **Other
Location** for any folder you pick. Under it, a five or ten second timer, the
microphone for a recording, whether to show the floating thumbnail, whether to
remember the last selection, whether to capture in HDR or SDR, and whether to
show the mouse pointer.

Turning the floating thumbnail off is the single best change in that menu if
you take a lot of them. With it on, every capture puts a card in the corner
that you either click or wait out.

## How to change the screenshot shortcuts

The four chords are rebindable, and the pane they live in is three levels down
where nobody finds it.

Open the Apple menu, choose **System Settings**, click **Keyboard** in the
sidebar, scroll to **Keyboard Shortcuts**, and click **Screenshots** in the
sidebar of the window that opens.

Five rows, each with a tick box and the chord beside it.

| Row                                            | Default |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Save picture of screen as a file               | ⇧⌘3     |
| Copy picture of screen to the clipboard        | ⌃⇧⌘3    |
| Save picture of selected area as a file        | ⇧⌘4     |
| Copy picture of selected area to the clipboard | ⌃⇧⌘4    |
| Screenshot and recording options               | ⇧⌘5     |

To change one, double-click the chord beside the row and press the keys you
want. The field takes the new combination as you press it, and clicking away
commits it. Untick a row to switch that capture off entirely, which frees the
chord for something else.

### The rebind most people want

Swap the two selected-area rows. Put ⇧⌘4 on **Copy picture of selected area to
the clipboard** and give **Save picture of selected area as a file** the longer
⌃⇧⌘4.

macOS ships them the other way round, so the chord in your fingers writes a
file every time and the Desktop fills up with captures you pasted into Slack
and never needed again. Swapped, the common case leaves nothing behind and you
hold one extra key on the rare occasion you want the PNG.

macOS will let you set the two rows to the same chord, and the one lower in the
list stops working with nothing on screen to say so. Set the second row before
you check the first.

### Two things that will not rebind

**Space and Option.** Pressing Space after the crosshair to capture a window,
and holding Option to drop the shadow, are built into the capture and are not
in this pane.

**The chord an app has already taken.** macOS does not warn you here. A
screenshot chord that an application has claimed for itself usually loses to
that application while it is in front, and you get no screenshot and no error.
If a rebind seems to do nothing, try it over the Desktop before you change it
again.

### If a recorder has taken ⇧⌘5

Apps that record the screen often hold that chord, because it is the one people
already press. [Prequel](/) does, and it switches the macOS binding off while
it holds it and switches it back on when you rebind the shortcut or quit the
app, so the toolbar comes back when you want it.

To have both, give one of them something else. Prequel's chord is in
**Settings** under Recording; the macOS toolbar's is the **Screenshot and
recording options** row above.

## What Markup gives you

Click the floating thumbnail, or open the PNG in Preview and press the pen
icon, and you get Markup: a pen, shapes, a text box, a signature, and a shape
and text colour.

It draws arrows and boxes, and that is genuinely most of what a screenshot
needs. Three things it has no answer for.

**No redaction.** Markup has no blur and no pixelate. People cover an email
address with a filled black rectangle, which is correct, and then people cover
it with a semi-transparent one, which is not. There is no tool here that
removes the pixels underneath.

**No background.** A screenshot of a window is a window on nothing. A
changelog entry or a landing page wants it sitting on something, with padding
around it and a shadow under it, and Markup has no concept of a canvas larger
than the picture.

**Nothing is reusable.** Every mark is drawn from scratch at whatever size you
happen to draw it, so the arrow on today's screenshot is a different weight
from yesterday's.

## How to take a screenshot in Prequel

[Prequel](/) is a screen recorder that also takes stills, because the two are
the same capture and the same editor. The switch is at the left of the panel.

![The recording dock, set up to take a screenshot](/docs/dock-setup-photo.webp)

### 1. Pick Screenshot, or choose it on the window

Press ⇧⌘5 to bring the panel up and press the Screenshot half of the switch.
You can also skip the panel: point at a window and pick **Screenshot** on the
window itself, which is the quicker route when you already know what you are
capturing.

### 2. It opens in the editor, already composed

A screenshot lands in the editor the way a recording does, with a background
behind it, padding around it, a border and a shadow. The same backgrounds, the
same frame presets, and the same lean: a screenshot can tilt up to 45 degrees
if a flat rectangle is too plain for where it is going.

Everything about the look is stored as a fraction of the frame's shorter edge,
so switching from 16:9 to a 4:5 portrait keeps the proportions. The picture is
re-composed, and no edge is lost to a crop.

### 3. Draw on it

Arrows, lines, boxes, ellipses, freehand, a highlighter and text. Each mark is
sized against the picture, so an arrow on a 5K capture reads the same as one on
a small crop.

Text comes in five styles: Classic, Modern, Neon, Typewriter and Strong. Pick
one and every label on the picture matches.

### 4. Hide what should not be in it

Drag a mask over the region and choose blur or pixelate. The exported PNG is
drawn from that canvas, so the original pixels are not in the file you hand
over, which is not true of most blurs.

This is the part worth the thirty seconds. A screenshot of your own dashboard
has a real customer's name in it more often than you would like.

### 5. Point at one thing

Two ways. A **loupe** magnifies a region in place, the way a reading glass
would. A **soft-focus lens** does the opposite and lets everything outside it
fall away, which works better on a dense interface where a magnified box reads
as a second screenshot pasted on top.

### 6. Export, or share it

Export writes a PNG and saves it straight away. Screenshots live in their own
Screenshots tab, separate from recordings, and anything you export lands in
Exports with everything else.

A share link on a screenshot opens the picture itself, so nobody arrives at an
empty video player.
