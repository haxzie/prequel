## What is a screen recorder for YouTube?

A screen recorder for YouTube is one that can export at the resolution and frame
rate YouTube rewards, keep small text legible through its encoder, and produce a
file with nothing stamped on it. In practice that means 4K export, at least 60
frames a second, and no watermark on whichever plan you are on.

Plenty of recorders capture a screen perfectly well and still produce a video
that looks soft once YouTube has re-encoded it. That is almost always a
resolution or frame-rate decision made at capture time, not something you can
fix afterwards.

YouTube is unforgiving about screen recordings in a way that other places are
not. A clip that looks fine in Slack turns to mush after YouTube's encoder gets
hold of it, and the reasons are specific and fixable.

This is what to do at capture time so the video survives the upload, and what to
do afterwards so people watch it.

## Record at 4K even if you publish at 1080p

This is the single highest-value setting on this page.

YouTube gives 4K uploads a better codec and a much higher bitrate than 1080p
ones, and that advantage is passed through to viewers watching at 1080p. A 4K
upload that people watch in 1080p genuinely looks better than a 1080p upload of
the same footage. Screen recordings are the hardest case for a video codec,
because they are full of hard edges and small text, which is exactly what
compression smears.

Record at your display's full resolution and export at 4K. If you cannot, export
at 1440p, which still gets you above the 1080p tier.

## Frame rate matters more than you think

Screen content moves in a specific way: it sits perfectly still, then something
scrolls or zooms fast. Thirty frames a second handles the still part fine and
falls apart on the fast part.

If your video has zooms in it, and a good screen recording does, 60 fps is the
floor. Higher is better for fast push-ins, which is where judder is most
visible. Many recorders in this category stop at 60; Prequel exports at up to
120 fps on every plan.

## The three things that lose viewers

### They cannot see what you are pointing at

At 1080p on a phone, a full-screen recording of a 3456-pixel display makes your
UI unreadable. Zooming in on each action is not a stylistic choice on YouTube,
it is the difference between a watchable video and one people leave.

[Screen recorders with automatic mouse zoom →](/blog/screen-recorder-mouse-zoom-mac)

### There is nobody in it

A face in the corner measurably increases retention, particularly in the first
thirty seconds. Ideally it is present when you are talking and small or gone
when the screen is dense, which needs a recorder that keeps the camera as its
own layer rather than burning it in.

[Screen recorders for Mac with a webcam overlay →](/blog/screen-recorder-webcam-overlay-mac)

### The audio is only your microphone

If your video is about software that makes sound, and the sound is missing,
viewers notice immediately. macOS will not record its own audio without help.

[How to record your Mac screen with internal audio →](/blog/record-mac-screen-with-internal-audio)

## A watermark makes it look like a trial

Several popular recorders stamp a logo on exports from their free tier or
trial. On YouTube that reads as "made with unpaid software" and it is the first
thing a comment mentions.

## Practical settings

| Setting    | Use                                                             |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Resolution | Record at display resolution, export at 4K (3840×2160)          |
| Frame rate | 60 fps minimum, higher if the video has fast zooms              |
| Codec      | H.264 for compatibility, HEVC for a smaller file at 4K          |
| Aspect     | 16:9 for the main video, 9:16 for a Shorts cut of the same take |
| Audio      | System audio and microphone on separate tracks                  |

## Shorts from the same recording

A landscape recording cropped to vertical usually loses the thing the video was
about. Reframing is different: you keep the whole recording and choose what sits
in the vertical frame, so the zoomed subject stays centred.

If your recorder has frame presets, one take produces both the main video and
the Shorts cut. If it does not, that is a second recording.
