macOS has no built-in way to record your screen and your webcam together.
QuickTime does one or the other, so every option below is either a third-party
app or a workaround.

Prequel sits at the top because it is what we would use, and it is worth being
straightforward: **Prequel is not free.** It is free for fourteen days and then

limits named. If free-forever is the requirement, start at number one.

## Can a Mac record the screen and camera at once?

Not on its own. QuickTime Player and the Cmd+Shift+5 toolbar each record a
screen capture or a camera movie, never both into a single file, so a picture-in
-picture recording always needs a third-party app on macOS.

The free apps that can do it differ in one way that matters later: whether the
camera is composited into the screen while recording, which makes its position
permanent, or kept as a separate track you can move afterwards.

## ★ Prequel

![The Prequel editor](/blog/tools/prequel.webp)

Every other tool here composites your camera into the screen while it records.
The bubble is in the corner, already round, already the size it will always be,
and that decision is permanent the moment you press stop.

Prequel records the screen, the camera, the microphone and system audio as
**four separate files**. The camera is kept at full resolution, so when the
recording opens in the editor you can move it, resize it, change its shape, put
a shadow under it or take it out entirely, with none of the quality loss that
cropping a burned-in bubble would cost you.

The rest of the pass is already made too: zooms that push in on what you clicked
and typed, a perspective tilt, and focus falling away from whatever the zoom is
aimed at. Export is 4K at up to 120 fps, no watermark, no limit on the length of
a take, rendered on your own Mac with nothing uploaded.

**Cost.** Fourteen days free with the whole app in it, then $29 once or $9 a
month.
**Requires.** An Apple Silicon Mac on macOS 14 or later.

## 1. OBS Studio

![OBS Studio's website](/blog/tools/obs.webp)

Free, open source, no watermark, no time limit, and it will record your screen
and webcam together at whatever quality your Mac can manage. It captures system
audio natively on Ventura and later, with no virtual driver.

The cost is setup. You build a scene, add a display capture source, add a video
capture device for the camera, position and crop it by hand, then set your
encoder and bitrate. Budget half an hour the first time. And once you have
recorded, the camera position is baked into the file, so moving it later means
recording again, which is the thing Prequel's separate files exist to avoid.

**Cost.** Free.

## 2. Cap

![Cap's website](/blog/tools/cap.webp)

The friendliest free option. Cap has proper camera layouts rather than a scene
you assemble yourself, an editor with automatic zoom and backgrounds, and it is
open source with the option to point it at your own storage.

The free tier stops at **five minutes a recording**, which is fine for a bug
report and not enough for a tutorial. Past that it is $29 a year for a
commercial desktop licence, which is worth reading carefully next to Prequel's
$29: Cap's buys twelve months and Prequel's buys the app.

**Cost.** Free for personal use, capped at five minutes. $29/year commercial,
or $12 per user per month for Cap Pro.

## 3. Loom

![Loom's website](/blog/tools/loom.webp)

Loom puts your camera in a bubble in the corner and gives you a link the moment
you stop. For sending a colleague a two-minute explanation it is still the
fastest thing here, and the free plan includes the camera.

The free plan also caps videos at **five minutes**, and the recording is the one
you performed: un-zoomed, uncut, camera wherever it was. Business is $18 per
user per month, twice Prequel's monthly rate, and the video lives on their page
rather than in your Downloads folder.

**Cost.** Free plan capped at five minutes, then $18–$24 per user per month.

## 4. QuickTime Player

![QuickTime Player's support page](/blog/tools/quicktime.webp)

Already installed, and worth naming precisely because so many guides get it
wrong: QuickTime records a **screen recording** or a **movie recording** from
your camera. Not both at once, and not in the same file.

The usual workaround is two recordings lined up in an editor afterwards, which
is more work than any other option here. It also cannot capture system audio
without a virtual driver.

**Cost.** Free with macOS.
