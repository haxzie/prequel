## What is trimming a screen recording?

Cutting footage off the front or the back of a take. You recorded yourself
finding the window, clearing your throat and then starting properly, and the
first eleven seconds have to go.

Cutting something out of the middle is a different operation, and it is the one
people actually need. Every screen recording has a stretch in it where you lost
your thread, and the built-in route on a Mac has no answer for it.

Which is why a three-minute walkthrough takes forty minutes. You trim the ends
in QuickTime, you find the bad thirty seconds in the middle, and you discover
that getting them out means exporting halves and joining them back together.

This is what QuickTime can and cannot cut, how to crop the picture as opposed
to the time, and the three keys that take a middle section out of a recording.

## What QuickTime gives you

The ends, and only the ends.

Open the recording, press **⌘T** or choose **Edit** then **Trim**. A yellow bar
appears around the timeline with a handle at each end. Drag them in, press
**Trim**, and save.

That is the whole feature. There is no split, no way to select a region in the
middle and delete it, and no second cut: once you have trimmed, the footage
outside the handles is gone and the handles are at the ends again.

The documented route to a middle cut is to trim a copy down to the first half,
trim another copy down to the second, and join them in iMovie. Two exports and
a re-encode to remove thirty seconds.

## Trimming is not cropping

Two different questions get asked with the same word, and the answers have
nothing in common.

**Trimming is time.** Shortening the recording.

**Cropping is picture.** Cutting the edges off the frame, usually because you
recorded a whole 5K display and the thing you were showing was a window in the
middle of it.

QuickTime cannot crop at all. iMovie can, by dropping the clip in and using
**Crop to Fill**, which scales the region you keep up to fill the frame and
costs you resolution doing it. The Photos app will crop a video on the same
terms.

The better answer to cropping is to not record the whole display. Both the ⇧⌘5
toolbar and every third-party recorder let you drag out a region before you
start, and a region captured at native resolution beats a crop scaled up from
one.

## How to trim and cut in Prequel

[Prequel](/) opens an editor on the take the moment you stop, with the
recording on a timeline as one clip, the filmstrip across it and the audio
waveform over that.

![The transport: the select and slice tools, the time, playback, and the things you can add](/docs/playback-controls.webp)

### 1. Drag an edge to trim an end

Drag either end of the clip inwards. The filmstrip moves with the edge, so you
see the frame you are cutting to as you drag it, which is the difference
between trimming to a number and trimming to a moment.

Hold **Command** and scroll, or pinch on the trackpad, to zoom the timeline in
first. Trimming to the frame on a three-minute take zoomed all the way out is
guesswork.

### 2. Split, click, delete

Three keys take a section out of the middle.

1. Move the playhead to where the cut starts and press **S**.
2. Move it to where the cut ends and press **S** again.
3. Click the part between the two and press **Backspace**.

The clips either side close up and the finished video runs straight through.
**Command + Z** puts any of it back.

Right-clicking a clip offers the same two as **Split here**, at the pointer,
and **Delete clip**.

### 3. Or use the slice tool

Press **C**, or click the scissors at the left of the transport, and every bar
on the timeline is cut where you click it: a clip, a zoom, or a text.

The bar under the pointer shows you the cut before you make it. The part that
would come away is washed red, and nothing lights up where the cut would leave
a piece too short to be worth having.

Press **V** or **Esc** to put it down and go back to selecting.

### 4. Cut by reading the transcript

This is the fastest route and it is the one worth changing your habits for.

If the take has a microphone track it is transcribed on your Mac when the
editor opens. Press **Edit captions** in the Captions panel and the transcript
opens as one paragraph. Select a run of words and the timeline lights up under
them: that band is the footage those words were spoken over.

Press Backspace. That stretch leaves the video, the clip is cut where the
sentence started and again where it ended, and everything after it closes up.

Read the transcript, delete the false start, delete the sentence where you lost
your thread, and watch the take get shorter. You are reading, not scrubbing, and
on a six-minute recording that is the difference between five minutes of work
and forty.

### 5. Change the frame if you need to crop

The frame is the picture's size, and it is a menu: Landscape 16:9, Landscape 4K,
Vertical 9:16, Square 1:1, Portrait 4:5, and the social sizes.

Change it late and the layout follows. Everything about the look is stored as a
fraction of the frame's shorter edge, so a camera at a third of the height
stays a third of the height when you switch a 16:9 recording to 9:16, and the
padding keeps its proportion. You get a re-composed vertical video, not a
centre crop with the edges missing.

To crop within the frame, click the screen to select it, then pull a corner to
resize it or hold **Option** while dragging to pan the picture inside its own
box.

## Three things worth knowing before you cut

**Cut on a sentence, not on a silence.** The temptation is to tighten every
gap. A screen recording with every pause removed reads as a sales video and is
harder to follow, because the viewer needs the half second where nothing
happens to catch up with what you clicked.

**Cut the setup, keep the result.** The dull part of a demo is you navigating to
the thing. Cut the navigation, keep the moment the thing works.

**Zoom the timeline before you trim.** Every cut that lands a third of a second
late was made at the wrong zoom level.
