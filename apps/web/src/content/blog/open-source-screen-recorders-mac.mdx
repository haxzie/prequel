## What is an open source screen recorder?

An open source screen recorder is one whose source code is published under a
licence that lets you read, modify and redistribute it. In practice that means
three things people actually care about: it is free, nobody can add a watermark
or a paywall in a future update without you being able to fork it, and you can
verify for yourself that your recordings are not being uploaded anywhere.

Licences differ, and the difference matters if you are shipping something built
on one. GPL and AGPL require you to publish changes you distribute; MIT does not.

## ★ Prequel

![The Prequel editor](/blog/tools/prequel.webp)

Every recorder on this list hands you the raw take. Prequel hands you the
finished video.

It watches where you click and type while it captures, then opens the editor
with the whole cinematic pass already made: zooms that push in on the work, a
perspective tilt so the frame has direction, focus falling away from whatever
the zoom is aimed at, and a camera you shape, size and place long after the take
is over. That last one works because it records the screen, camera, microphone
and system audio as separate files, so the camera stays full resolution and
re-framable rather than burned into a corner.

Everything renders on your own Mac, on Apple's own media engine. Nothing is
uploaded, nothing is queued, and nothing leaves the machine unless you ask for a
share link. Export runs to 4K at up to 120 fps with no watermark and no cap on
the length of a take.

**Licence.** Proprietary, and priced accordingly: $29 once, or $9 a month, with
fourteen days free.
**Requires.** An Apple Silicon Mac on macOS 14 or later.

## 1. OBS Studio

![OBS Studio's website](/blog/tools/obs.webp)

75,000 stars, GPL-2.0, and commits landing this week. OBS is the most capable
free recording tool on any platform and the only one here that is genuinely
industrial: multiple sources, scene composition, filters, bitrate control,
plugins, and live streaming to anything that speaks RTMP. On macOS Ventura and
later it captures system audio natively, with no virtual driver.

It is also the least pleasant way to make a two-minute product demo. There is no
editor, so the zooms, cuts and framing are all still ahead of you in another
app, which is the work Prequel finishes before you open it.

If an open licence is the requirement, this is the answer and nothing else on
this list is close on capability.

**Licence.** GPL-2.0. Free, no limits, no watermark.

## 2. Cap

![Cap's website](/blog/tools/cap.webp)

The open-source answer to Loom, and it has moved fast: 21,000 stars and commits
daily. Instant mode gives you a hosted link in seconds, Studio mode records
locally and opens a real editor with automatic zoom, backgrounds and camera
layouts. You can point it at your own S3 bucket or Google Drive so the files
stay yours, and it runs on Windows as well as macOS.

Two things to know. Its licence is a custom one rather than a standard OSI
licence, so read it if you are picking on licence terms specifically. And the
free tier stops at five minutes a recording, where a commercial desktop licence
is $29 billed yearly and Cap Pro is $12 per user per month. That first number
looks like Prequel's, so it is worth saying plainly: Cap's $29 buys a year and
Prequel's $29 buys the app.

**Licence.** Open source, custom terms. Free for personal use with a five-minute
cap; $29/year for a commercial desktop licence.

## 3. Screenity

![Screenity on GitHub](/blog/tools/screenity.webp)

18,000 stars, GPL-3.0, and actively developed. Screenity is a Chrome extension
rather than a Mac app, which is either exactly what you want or immediately
disqualifying. If your recordings are browser tabs, it is excellent: annotate
while you record, draw on the page, spotlight the cursor, blur sensitive areas,
and export without a time limit or a watermark.

Being an extension is the whole trade. It cannot record outside the browser, so
a native app demo is out, and everything happens at tab resolution rather than
at the 4K 120 fps Prequel exports.

**Licence.** GPL-3.0. Free, unlimited, no watermark.

## 4. QuickRecorder

![QuickRecorder's website](/blog/tools/quickrecorder.webp)

8,600 stars, AGPL-3.0, and the most Mac-native thing on this list after Prequel.
It is built directly on ScreenCaptureKit, records a window rather than a region
so a window stays clean when something floats over it, captures system audio,
and stays out of the way in the menu bar.

It is deliberately small. There is no editor at all, so the zooms and the cuts
are a separate job. Commits slowed through 2025, though the app works and is
widely used.

**Licence.** AGPL-3.0. Free.

## 5. Kap

![Kap's website](/blog/tools/kap.webp)

19,000 stars, MIT, and the nicest small tool here. Kap does one thing: short
captures exported to GIF, MP4, WebM or APNG, with click highlighting and
trimming. For a clip in a GitHub issue or a Slack thread it is still the fastest
route from screen to shareable file.

Worth checking before you commit to it: **the last commit was November 2024.**
It runs, and MIT means you can fork it, but nobody is shipping fixes. It also
tops out well below the 4K 120 fps Prequel exports, which matters the moment a
clip is going anywhere other than an issue tracker.

**Licence.** MIT. Free.

## So which one

If you need an open licence, take OBS Studio and accept that the editing happens
somewhere else. If your recordings live in a browser tab, Screenity. If you want
a hosted link and might self-host it later, Cap. If you want something small and
native, QuickRecorder. If you want GIFs for issues, Kap, with the caveat that it
is dormant.

If the licence was never really the point, and what you actually wanted was a
recording worth sending without an afternoon of editing, that is Prequel:

**Social clips.** Every frame preset is included, so a landscape recording
reframes for TikTok, Reels or Shorts without cropping the interesting half out.

**Tutorials and how-tos.** The zooms land on what you clicked and typed, already
placed rather than keyframed by hand.

**Product tours and demos.** Perspective tilt, depth of field and a camera you
frame after the take, with no cap on the length of a recording.

**YouTube.** 4K at up to 120 fps, never watermarked, rendered on your own Mac.

$29 once, which is less than a month of most of the paid tools in this category,
and fourteen days free before you decide.
