People leave Loom for one of three reasons, and which one applies decides where

**The price went up and the free plan is thin.** Business is $18 per user per

## What is Loom, and why look for an alternative?

Loom is a screen recorder built around speed of sharing: you record, it uploads,
and you paste a link. For sending a colleague a two-minute walkthrough it is
still the fastest thing in the category, and that is genuinely what it is good
at.

People look for alternatives for three reasons. The free plan caps videos at
five minutes, Business is $18 per user per month, and the video lives on Loom's
servers as a page rather than a file you own. Which of those applies to you
decides where you should go.

---

## 1. Prequel

![The Prequel editor](/blog/tools/prequel.webp)

The closest thing to "Loom, but the video is actually good."

Prequel records locally and hands back a video that is already directed: zooms
that push in on what you clicked and typed, a perspective tilt so the frame has
direction, focus falling away from whatever the zoom is aimed at, and a camera
you shape, size and place after the take rather than before it. That last one
works because it writes the screen, camera, microphone and system audio as four
separate files, so the camera is never burned into a corner.

There is no cap on the length of a recording on any plan, against Loom's five
minutes on free. Nothing is uploaded unless you ask for a share link, so the
export is a file you own rather than a page you rent. And it is $9 a month
against Loom's $18, or $29 once and never again.

**Price.** $29 once, or $9 a month, with fourteen days free.
**Requires.** An Apple Silicon Mac on macOS 14 or later.

## 2. Cap

![Cap's website](/blog/tools/cap.webp)

The open-source Loom. Instant mode gives you a hosted link in seconds, which is
the Loom workflow almost exactly, and Studio mode records locally with an editor
that has automatic zoom and backgrounds. You can point it at your own S3 bucket
or Google Drive, which is the answer if "our video is on their servers" was the
reason you left.

The free tier caps recordings at five minutes, the same limit you were escaping,
and export tops at 4K 60. A commercial desktop licence is $29 a year: the same
figure as Prequel's one-off, buying twelve months rather than the app.

**Price.** Free up to five minutes, then $29/year or $12 per user per month.

## 3. Tella

![Tella's website](/blog/tools/tella.webp)

Tella is the closest to Loom in shape: record in the browser, get a branded
landing page, no install. For customer-facing demos where the page matters as
much as the video, it is a genuine step up from a Loom link.

Two things to check. Sixty fps export is reserved for the $19 Premium tier,
where Prequel exports at up to 120 fps on every plan including the $29 one-off.
And the video still lives on a hosted page, so if leaving Loom was about owning
the file, this does not solve it.

[Prequel vs Tella →](/alternatives/tella)

**Price.** Pro $13, Premium $19 per user per month. No free plan, seven-day trial.

## 4. Screen Studio

![Screen Studio's website](/blog/tools/screen-studio.webp)

If you left Loom because the recordings looked raw, Screen Studio is the
obvious destination: automatic zoom on clicks and the smoothest cursor movement
in the category.

It is macOS only, it has no sharing layer to replace Loom's links, and it
exports at 4K 60 against Prequel's 4K 120, without the perspective tilt or the
depth of field. Its $29 is monthly; the yearly rate works out at $9 a month,
which is what Prequel charges monthly.

[Prequel vs Screen Studio →](/alternatives/screen-studio)

**Price.** $29/mo, or $9/mo billed yearly.

## 5. Descript

![Descript's website](/blog/tools/descript.webp)

Different tool for a different problem. Descript transcribes your recording and
lets you cut the video by deleting words, which for talking-head content is
genuinely a better way to work than a timeline.

Your media lives on their servers as a condition of the product working, so it
does not solve the ownership complaint, and the free tier watermarks exports and
caps them at 720p. Paid plans start around $16 a month, above Prequel's monthly
rate and well above its one-off.

[Prequel vs Descript →](/alternatives/descript)

**Price.** Free tier, then roughly $16 to $50 a month.

## 6. OBS Studio

![OBS Studio's website](/blog/tools/obs.webp)

If the price was the whole problem, OBS is free forever, open source, never
watermarks and has no time limit. It records screen and webcam together and
captures system audio natively on Ventura and later.

There is no sharing layer and no editor, so the link and the edit are both jobs
you take elsewhere. That edit is the part Prequel has already finished by the
time it opens the file.

**Price.** Free.

## So which one

If you left over price alone, OBS Studio. If you want the Loom workflow with
your own storage underneath it, Cap. If you need branded pages for customers,
Tella. If you mostly cut talking heads, Descript.

If you left because the videos were not good enough to send to customers, that
is what Prequel is for:

**Product tours and demos.** Zooms already placed on what you clicked, a
perspective tilt, and no cap on the length of a take, against Loom's five
minutes on free.

**Tutorials and how-tos.** The camera resized per section after the take rather
than a fixed bubble in one corner for the whole video.

**Social clips.** Every frame preset is included, so a landscape recording
reframes for vertical without cropping the subject out.

**YouTube.** 4K at up to 120 fps, no watermark on any plan, rendered on your own
Mac rather than queued on someone's server.

$9 a month against Loom's $18, or $29 once. Fourteen days free.
