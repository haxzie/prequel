## What is the screen recording shortcut on a Mac?

Shift + Command + 5. It opens the Screenshot toolbar along the bottom of your
screen, where you pick the whole display or a region you drag out, and then
press Record.

No key on a Mac starts a recording by itself. ⇧⌘5 opens the toolbar, and the
recording begins on the click after that.

That gap is why the shortcut feels broken the first time you use it. You learn
the chord, you press it, and you are still two clicks and a drag away from
recording anything. Then you want to stop, and the key you reach for is not
bound to anything either.

This is every key macOS gives you for capturing a screen, what each one
actually does, and the ones Prequel adds so a take starts and stops on one
chord.

## What macOS binds

Four chords, all of them under the Screenshot service.

| Keys                | Does                                                                         |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shift + Command + 5 | Opens the Screenshot toolbar: record a display or a region, or take a still. |
| Shift + Command + 3 | Saves a picture of every display straight away.                              |
| Shift + Command + 4 | Drag out a region and it saves a picture of that.                            |
| Shift + Command + 6 | Saves a picture of the Touch Bar, on a Mac that has one.                     |

Two modifiers are worth knowing. Press Space after ⇧⌘4 and the crosshair
becomes a camera that captures the window under it, with the shadow. Hold
Control with any of the four and the picture goes on the clipboard and no file
is written.

## Stopping a recording

Command + Control + Esc stops a recording started from the ⇧⌘5 toolbar. The
stop button in the menu bar does the same thing, and it is the one most people
find, because ⌘⌃Esc is also the Force Quit chord on an older Mac and nobody
trusts it.

QuickTime Player has no global shortcut at all. You start a recording from
**File**, then **New Screen Recording**, and you stop it from the menu bar or
by pressing ⌘⌃Esc.

## What the built-in shortcuts cannot do

They open a toolbar. That is the whole of it, and four things follow from it.

**You cannot start a take from inside the app you are recording.** The toolbar
takes focus, so the window you were about to demonstrate loses it, and the
first second of the recording is you clicking back into it.

**There is no pause.** A macOS screen recording runs from Record to stop. If
the phone goes, that is in the file.

**There is no shortcut for the camera.** ⇧⌘5 records a display or a region.
Your face is a separate QuickTime movie, made separately.

**Nothing is bound in the editing half**, because there is no editing half. You
get a `.mov` on the Desktop and whatever you open it with.

## The shortcuts in Prequel

[Prequel](/) takes ⇧⌘5 over. macOS gives that chord to its own toolbar, so
Prequel switches the system binding off while it holds it and switches it back
on when you rebind the shortcut or quit the app.

![The Settings window, where the start and stop shortcut is rebound](/docs/settings.webp)

### 1. Shift + Command + 5 starts and stops

One chord, both directions. The app you are recording keeps focus, so there is
no click to trim off the front, and pressing it again ends the take and opens
the editor on it.

To move it, open **Settings** and set **Shortcut** under Recording. Prequel
stores the chord in one place and every surface that draws it reads that, so
the tray menu and the dock always show the key you actually bound.

### 2. Shift + Command + P pauses

Pause and resume mid-take, from any application. The phone goes, you press
⇧⌘P, you deal with it, you press ⇧⌘P again. The pause is not in the file.

**Esc** cancels while you are choosing a source, and cancels the countdown if
you change your mind in the three seconds after you press record.

### 3. The editor keys

The editor opens on the take you just made. These are the keys worth learning,
and they are ignored while you are typing in a field.

| Keys                  | Does                                             |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Space                 | Play or pause.                                   |
| S                     | Split the clip at the playhead.                  |
| C                     | The slice tool: click any bar to cut it there.   |
| V                     | The select tool: pick clips, zooms and texts up. |
| Z                     | Add a zoom at the playhead.                      |
| Backspace             | Delete the selected clip, zoom or text.          |
| Command + Z           | Undo.                                            |
| Command + scroll      | Zoom the timeline in and out. Pinch does it too. |
| Option while dragging | Pan the picture inside its box.                  |
| Shift while resizing  | Keep the proportions.                            |

S then S then Backspace is how a middle section leaves a recording: split where
the cut starts, split where it ends, click the part between them and delete it.

### 4. The teleprompter keys

While the prompter island is showing, whatever application is in front.

| Keys                     | Does              |
| ------------------------ | ----------------- |
| Control + Option + Down  | On a sentence.    |
| Control + Option + Up    | Back a sentence.  |
| Control + Option + Space | Pause and resume. |
| Control + Option + 0     | Back to the top.  |

Control and Option together, because those two are free in most applications.
A prompter advanced with a key your code editor also uses is a prompter that
scrolls your file.
