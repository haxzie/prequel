macOS has no built-in picture-in-picture for screen recordings. QuickTime and
the Cmd+Shift+5 toolbar record your screen or your camera, never both into one
file, so every option here is a third-party app.

The thing that separates them is not whether they can put your face on screen.
They all can, except QuickTime. It is **whether that decision is still yours
after you stop recording.**

## What is a webcam overlay?

A webcam overlay is your camera shown on top of a screen recording, usually as a
circle or rounded rectangle in a corner. It is what makes a tutorial feel like a
person explaining something rather than a disembodied cursor, and it measurably
increases how far people watch.

macOS cannot produce one on its own, so this is always a third-party app. The
apps differ in whether the camera is burned into the video while recording or
kept as a separate layer you can move, resize and reshape afterwards.

## 1. Prequel

![The Prequel editor](/blog/tools/prequel.webp)

Every other recorder here composites your camera into the screen while it
captures. The bubble is in the corner, already round, already the size it will
always be, and that is permanent the moment you press stop. If it covers
something important in the third minute, you record again.

Prequel writes the screen, the camera, the microphone and system audio as **four
separate files** with a manifest. The camera is kept at full resolution, so when
the editor opens you can move it, resize it, change its shape, put a shadow
under it, shrink it for the dense part of the demo and grow it for the piece to
camera, or take it out entirely. None of that costs quality, because nothing was
ever burned in.

The rest of the pass is already made: zooms that push in on what you clicked and
typed, a perspective tilt, and focus falling away from whatever the zoom is
aimed at while the camera stays sharp. Export runs to 4K at up to 120 fps with
no watermark and no cap on the length of a take, rendered on your own Mac.

**Camera.** Recorded separately, framed, shaped and sized afterwards.
**Price.** $29 once, or $9 a month, with fourteen days free.
**Requires.** An Apple Silicon Mac on macOS 14 or later.

## 2. Screen Studio

![Screen Studio's website](/blog/tools/screen-studio.webp)

Screen Studio's camera handling is good and its automatic zoom is the
best-known version of the effect. You pick a shape and a corner, and the result
looks deliberate.

The framing is a decision you make before you record, and it exports at 4K 60
where Prequel runs to 4K 120. On price, note that $29 is the monthly rate and
their yearly one works out at $9 a month, which is Prequel's monthly rate.

**Price.** $29/mo, or $9/mo billed yearly.

## 3. Loom

![Loom's website](/blog/tools/loom.webp)

Loom's camera bubble is the format most people picture when they think of a
screen recording with a face on it, and the free plan includes it. Record, stop,
paste a link.

The free plan caps videos at **five minutes**, the bubble stays where it started,
and the recording is the one you performed: un-zoomed and uncut. Business is $18
per user per month, twice Prequel's monthly rate, and the video lives on Loom's
page rather than as a file you own.

[Prequel vs Loom →](/alternatives/loom)

**Price.** Free up to five minutes, then $18–$24 per user per month.

## 4. Cap

![Cap's website](/blog/tools/cap.webp)

Cap gives you real camera layouts rather than a scene you assemble, plus an
editor with automatic zoom and backgrounds, and it is open source with the
option to use your own storage.

The free tier stops recordings at **five minutes**. A commercial desktop licence
is $29 a year, which is the same number as Prequel's one-off and a different
thing: Cap's buys twelve months, Prequel's buys the app. The camera layout is
also chosen up front rather than adjusted afterwards.

**Price.** Free up to five minutes, then $29/year or $12 per user per month.

## 5. OBS Studio

![OBS Studio's website](/blog/tools/obs.webp)

OBS will absolutely put your webcam over your screen, at whatever size, shape
and position you like, for free and with no watermark or time limit. It captures
system audio natively on Ventura and later.

The cost is half an hour of setup the first time: a display capture source, a
video capture device, cropping and positioning by hand, then encoder settings.
And once recorded, the camera is baked into the file, so a bubble that covers
the wrong thing means recording again.

**Price.** Free.

## 6. QuickTime Player

![QuickTime Player's support page](/blog/tools/quicktime.webp)

Worth naming precisely, because a lot of guides are vague about it: QuickTime
does a **screen recording** or a **movie recording** from your camera. Not both
at once, and not into one file.

The workaround is recording twice and lining them up in an editor afterwards,
which is more work than any other option here, and it still cannot capture
system audio without a virtual driver.

**Price.** Free with macOS.

## So which one

If free is the requirement and your recordings are short, Cap. If you want a
link in ten seconds for a colleague, Loom. If you want free with no limits and
will spend the setup time, OBS Studio.

If the video is going in front of customers, the camera being editable after the
fact is worth more than it sounds, and that is Prequel:

**Tutorials and how-tos.** Shrink the camera for the dense parts and grow it for

**Product tours and demos.** Zooms, tilt and depth of field already applied,

**Social clips.** Every frame preset is included, so a landscape recording with
a camera in it reframes vertical without cropping your face out of the shot.

**YouTube.** 4K at up to 120 fps, no watermark on any plan or during the trial,
rendered on your own Mac.

Fourteen days free, then $29 once.
