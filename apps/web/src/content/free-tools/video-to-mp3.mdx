## ffmpeg, in your browser

This page is ffmpeg compiled to WebAssembly. The same program that every video
tool on your machine uses underneath runs here instead, inside the tab, on your
own processor. Your video is read from your disk by the page, decoded, and the
audio written out as an MP3.

That is the whole difference between this and the converters that rank above it.
They upload your file, put it in a queue on a server you know nothing about,
convert it there and give you a link that expires. Nothing is uploaded here,
because there is no code in the page that could upload it.

## What it costs

The encoder is a 32 MB download, once per visit. That is the honest trade: a
server-side converter sends a kilobyte of HTML and does the work somewhere else,
and this one sends the converter to you. After the first run it is cached, so a
second file starts immediately.

Conversion then runs at the speed of your own machine on one thread, which for
pulling an audio track out is roughly real time or better. A twenty minute
recording takes a minute or two.

## 192 kbps, and why

The MP3 is written at 192 kbps at the video's own sample rate. For speech, a
lecture, a podcast or a screen recording that is past the point where more
bitrate changes anything you can hear, and it keeps a half hour recording under
30 MB, which still attaches to an email.

Nothing about the original is touched. The video on your disk is the video you
started with.

## If you are recording to publish

Extracting audio afterwards is a repair job. Prequel records your Mac's screen
with the microphone and the system audio on separate tracks and separate gains,
so a notification landing over your voice is a slider rather than a reason to
start again, and the audio is already apart from the picture when you open the
editor.
