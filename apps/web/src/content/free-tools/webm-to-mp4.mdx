## WebM is a browser format

It holds VP8 or VP9 video and Opus audio, all of which browsers decode and most
other things do not. Premiere and Final Cut will not import a WebM. Older
iPhones, smart TVs and set-top boxes will not play one. That is the whole reason
this page exists.

An MP4 holds H.264 and AAC, which has been decoded in hardware on every device
made this century.

## It is a real re-encode

Nothing inside a WebM can be lifted into an MP4 untouched, so every frame is
decoded and encoded again as H.264 at CRF 23, with the audio re-encoded to AAC
at 160 kbps. That takes about the length of the clip on a current laptop,
because the encoder is WebAssembly on a single thread.

The MP4 is written with its index at the front, so a file you post or send
starts playing before it has finished downloading.

## Where your WebM came from

Almost always a browser. Chrome and Firefox record to WebM, so anything captured
by a web-based recorder, a meeting tool or a screen recorder extension arrives
in this format, and then will not import into the editor you wanted to use.

## Nothing leaves the tab

The conversion runs in the same browser that made the file. ffmpeg compiled to
WebAssembly reads it, converts it and writes the MP4 back, with no upload and no
queue.

If you are on a Mac and tired of the round trip, Prequel records the screen
straight to MP4 at up to 4K, with the zooms already placed when the editor
opens.
