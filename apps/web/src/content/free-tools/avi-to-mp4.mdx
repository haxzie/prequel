## Why an AVI has to be re-encoded

An AVI is a container from 1992, and what is usually inside it is DivX or Xvid.
An MP4 cannot hold either. So unlike a MOV, which is normally rewrapped in a
second, an AVI is decoded frame by frame and encoded again as H.264 with its
audio re-encoded to AAC.

That takes about as long as the clip itself on a current laptop, because the
encoder is WebAssembly running on one thread. A three minute dash cam clip is a
few minutes. An hour of footage is better converted on your machine with ffmpeg
or Handbrake.

## The file usually gets smaller

H.264 is two codec generations ahead of what your AVI is carrying, so the same
picture needs roughly half the bits at the same quality. CRF 23 is the setting
used here, which is the one people use for delivery rather than for archiving.

## Where AVI files come from

Dash cams, old camcorders, security recorders, capture software from the DVD
era, and downloads from the 2000s. They play in VLC and almost nowhere else: no
phone decodes DivX, no browser plays an AVI, and most editors will not import
one.

## It stays on your machine

Old AVI files are usually personal footage, and most converters that rank for
this phrase upload yours to a server. This page runs ffmpeg in the tab, so the
file is converted on your own processor and never sent anywhere.
