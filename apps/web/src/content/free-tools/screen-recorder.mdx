## Everything happens in the tab

Your browser already has the three parts a screen recorder needs. Screen capture
hands over a live picture of a display, a window or a tab. A canvas draws that
picture into the frame you picked, with your camera in the corner. MediaRecorder
encodes the result into a file. This page wires the three together and gives you
the controls.

That is why there is no sign up and no upload. The recording is made on your
machine, held in your browser's memory while it runs, and saved to your
downloads folder when you ask for it. Close the tab and it is gone.

## What the controls change

The preview is the file. The frame, the camera bubble and the two levels are all
part of what gets encoded, so the take you watch before you press record is the
take you download.

Frame comes first. Match source keeps the proportions of whatever you shared,
and the four presets reframe it: 1080p for YouTube, 9:16 for a Short or a Reel,
square for a feed, 4:5 for a post. The camera's size is set as a proportion of
the frame, so switching from landscape to vertical keeps it where you put it.

Sound is two inputs on one file. Your microphone is one, the shared screen's own
audio is the other, and each has a level that keeps working while the recording
runs. Chrome and Edge are the browsers that offer screen audio, and only if you
tick Share audio in the picker.

## Thirty minutes a take

A browser recording lives in the tab until you save it, so a long take is a
growing block of memory in a page that is also compositing video. The tool stops
by itself at thirty minutes and hands you the file, which is the version of that
limit that leaves you with a recording.

For anything longer, anything at 4K, or anything you want zooms, backgrounds and
a timeline for, record on your Mac with Prequel.
