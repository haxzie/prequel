## This is a remux, not a conversion

An MKV from OBS is almost always H.264 video and AAC audio, which is exactly
what an MP4 holds. So the streams are copied from the Matroska container into an
MP4 one, byte for byte. It takes about a second, nothing is re-encoded and
nothing is lost. It is the same operation OBS's own "Remux Recordings" performs.

Where the MKV holds something an MP4 cannot carry, the page re-encodes to H.264
instead and takes considerably longer.

## Why OBS records MKV at all

Because an MP4 is written with its index at the end. If the recording is
interrupted, by a crash, a full disk or a power cut, that index is never written
and the file is often unreadable. Matroska survives it. OBS's advice is to
record to MKV and remux afterwards, which is the step most people skip until
their editor refuses the file.

## What gets dropped

The first video track and the first audio track are kept. Matroska can carry
several audio languages and a stack of subtitle streams; an MP4 is not usually
expected to, and they do not survive the remux.

## It stays on your machine

ffmpeg runs in this tab, so a gameplay recording, a captured call or an internal
demo is converted on your own processor and never uploaded.

Gameplay files are often far larger than this page's 300 MB ceiling, which is
the tab's memory rather than a policy. Those are better remuxed in OBS itself.
