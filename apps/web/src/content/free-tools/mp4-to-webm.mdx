## Only convert if something is asking for it

Every browser has played H.264 in an MP4 for years, so WebM is not a
compatibility upgrade. It is what you produce when a particular place insists on
a royalty-free codec: Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons, some open-source project
sites, and the occasional web app that validates uploads by codec.

If nothing is asking, keep the MP4.

## VP8, and why not VP9

The video is encoded as VP8 at 1 Mbit with Vorbis audio. VP9 would make a
smaller file and takes roughly four times as long to encode, which in a tab, on
one thread, is the difference between waiting and closing it. Every browser that
decodes VP9 also decodes VP8.

This is the slowest conversion on the site. A clip of a minute or two is the
right size for it; anything longer belongs in ffmpeg or Handbrake on your
machine.

## The file will not necessarily shrink

At this bitrate a WebM usually lands close to the MP4 it came from, sometimes
larger. Size is not the reason to do this. Compatibility with one specific
destination is.

## It runs in this tab

ffmpeg compiled to WebAssembly does the encoding on your own processor. The MP4
is never uploaded, which matters when it is an unreleased product video on its
way to a public wiki.
