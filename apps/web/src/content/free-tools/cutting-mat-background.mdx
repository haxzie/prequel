## The mat, drawn to your frame

A self-healing cutting mat is a ruled grid, numbered rulers down all four edges
and a fan of angles in one corner. Everybody recognises it, and a photograph of
one is the wrong file for a background: it is a fixed number of pixels, so it
arrives at a different shape stretched and half cropped away.

This page draws the mat at the size you ask for. Pick a 16:9 display or a 9:16
phone and the grid grows columns to suit, the rulers renumber, and the arcs in
the corner stay circles.

## The fan is the part that matters

Rays at 15, 30, 45, 60 and 75 degrees, three arcs with a nick every five
degrees, and the angles written on the first arc. Those are the parts that go
wrong when a picture of a mat is stretched: a 45 degree ray becomes 51 degrees
and the arcs become ellipses. Drawn at your frame's own size, they are the
angles they say they are.

## Rulers on all four edges

Numbered every five cells, with a long tick on the number and a short one
between. Zero sits in the corner the fan comes out of, so the grid, both rulers
and the angles all agree about where the origin is. The Grid slider sets how
many cells fit across the short edge, from 14 to 72.

## Eight colours

Olive is the mat everybody pictures. Slate, Carbon and Blueprint are the ones
that sit under a screenshot without competing with it, and Paper is the only
palette here where the lines are darker than the ground.

## Behind a recording

Prequel ships this mat as the default background in its editor, redrawn at the
export's own resolution. Record your Mac's screen, and the editor opens with
the zooms already placed, your camera framed and the mat behind it.
