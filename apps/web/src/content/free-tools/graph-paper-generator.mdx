## Squares, at the size you need them

Graph paper is a flat ground and two sets of lines. The hard part is getting it
at the size of the thing you are putting it behind, which is why this page
draws it on request rather than handing you a tiling image. Pick 1080p and you
get 1920 by 1080. Pick the 6K display and you get 6016 by 3384, with the same
number of squares across it.

That is what the Grid slider sets: squares across the short edge, from 14 to 72.
Every distance in the drawing is a fraction of that edge, so a wallpaper for a
phone and one for a Pro Display are the same picture at two sizes.

## Every fifth line is heavier

A plain grid of identical lines is hard to count. This one bands at five, the
way engineering paper does, and the bands are counted from the bottom left
corner. Five small squares make one big one, and you can read off a distance
without touching a ruler.

## PNG or SVG

The PNG is painted in this tab, at the size in the dock, and saved straight to
your downloads folder. Use it as a desktop wallpaper, a slide background or the
ground behind a screenshot.

The SVG is the better file if anything will resize it again. It is two paths
and a rectangle, so Figma, Illustrator, Sketch and any browser will scale it to
any size with the hairlines still hairlines. It is also a few kilobytes.

## Behind a recording

Prequel ships this grid as one of seven drawn backgrounds in its editor, where
it is redrawn at the export's own resolution. Record your Mac's screen, and the
editor opens with the zooms already placed, your camera framed and a background
behind it.
