## Hairlines at forty-five degrees

Hatching is the oldest way to shade a drawing: parallel lines, close together,
at an angle. This page draws them across the whole frame at 45 degrees, with
every fifth line picked out in the heavier colour so the field has a rhythm
rather than a single flat texture.

The spacing is measured perpendicular to the lines, which is the gap your eye
reads. The Grid slider sets it against the short edge of the frame, so a hatch
drawn for a phone and one drawn for a 6K display have lines the same distance
apart relative to the picture.

## Tight or open

At 72 the field is nearly solid and reads as a tone. At 14 it is a loose set of
rules you can put text over. The heavy line every fifth one keeps both ends of
that range from reading as noise.

## PNG or SVG

The PNG is painted in this tab at the size in the dock. The SVG is two paths,
which is the file to take into Figma or Illustrator: scale it to anything,
recolour it in two clicks, and the hairlines stay hairlines.

## Behind a recording

Prequel ships this hatch as one of seven drawn backgrounds in its editor,
redrawn at the export's own resolution. Record your Mac's screen, and the editor
opens with the zooms already placed, your camera framed and a background behind
it.
