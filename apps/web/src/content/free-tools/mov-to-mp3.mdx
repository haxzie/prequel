## A screen recording is mostly picture

A one hour Mac screen recording is often several hundred megabytes, and almost
all of that is the screen. The audio inside it is about 25 MB. If what you need
is the conversation, the meeting, the lecture or the voiceover, the MP3 is the
file worth keeping and sending.

## How it works here

This page is ffmpeg compiled to WebAssembly. The QuickTime container is opened
in the tab, the audio track is decoded out of it, and an MP3 is written at
192 kbps at the recording's own sample rate, which for a Mac recording is
48 kHz.

Nothing is uploaded. The MOV is read from your disk and the MP3 is written back
to it, and no server is involved at any point. For a recording of a customer
call or an internal tool, that is not a detail.

## When there is no audio at all

QuickTime's screen recorder captures the microphone and nothing else unless you
have routed system audio into it. A recording made with the microphone off has
no audio track, so there is nothing to extract and the page says so rather than
handing back an empty file.

## Transcribing it afterwards

An MP3 is what most transcription services want, and this is the quickest way to
make one. If the recording is still on your Mac, Prequel transcribes it there,
with the model running locally, and writes the captions onto the video rather
than into a separate document.
