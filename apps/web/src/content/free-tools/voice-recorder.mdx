## A recorder, and a meter you can trust

The meter is the reason this page exists. It reads the microphone you selected,
from the moment it opens, and it follows the level slider, so the bars moving
are the bars being recorded. Speak at the volume you will use: bars that barely
move mean the level is too low, bars pinned at the ends mean it is too high.

Both are a drag of a slider before you start, and neither is fixable once the
take is recorded.

## Pause is part of one file

Pause holds the recording and Resume carries on into the same file, so a take
with three gaps in it is still one download. The clock counts recorded time
rather than the time you spent thinking.

Nothing leaves the tab. Your microphone goes through an audio graph in your
browser and into your browser's own encoder, and the result sits in memory until
you download it. There is no account, no transcript and no upload.

## The file

M4A with AAC in Chrome, Edge and Safari, WebM with Opus in Firefox, both at 128
kbps. That is past the point where speech gains anything from more bitrate, and
both open in every editor and attach to any email.

Takes run up to thirty minutes. Audio is small, but the recording is held in the
tab until you save it, so the same ceiling applies as to the video tools.

## Recording a voiceover for a video

Record here, then put the audio under your footage in whatever you edit in. If
the video is a screen recording, Prequel on a Mac records your microphone and
your system audio on separate tracks with separate gains, and transcribes the
take locally to write captions onto it.
