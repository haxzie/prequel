## What is actually inside an MP4

An MP4 is a box with two things in it: a video track, almost always H.264, and
an audio track, almost always AAC. Converting one to MP3 means throwing the
first away and re-encoding the second. That is why a 400 MB recording of an hour
long call comes back at around 25 MB, and why it takes seconds rather than
minutes: nothing has to touch the picture.

## It runs here, not on a server

This page is ffmpeg compiled to WebAssembly. Your MP4 is read off your disk by
the browser, decoded in the tab and written back to your downloads folder. No
upload, no queue, no link in an email, and no server that has a copy of your
file for a fortnight.

The cost is a 32 MB download of the encoder, once per visit. After that every
file starts immediately.

## 192 kbps

The MP3 is written at 192 kbps at whatever sample rate the MP4 carried. For a
call, a lecture, a podcast or a screen recording that is past the point where
more bitrate changes anything audible, and it keeps an hour under 30 MB.

It is a second lossy pass, because the AAC in the MP4 was already compressed.
On speech that is inaudible. On music you intend to master, keep the original
and work from it.
