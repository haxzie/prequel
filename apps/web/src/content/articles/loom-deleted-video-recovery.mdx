The short answer is no. Loom deletes a video immediately and permanently, documents no trash and no retention window, and says its own Support cannot bring one back.

That is a different case from a recording lost to a crash before it ever uploaded, where there may still be a file on your Mac. If that is what happened to you, [Loom crashed and lost your recording](/articles/loom-lost-recording-recovery) is the page you want.

## What Loom documents

"Permanent video deletion is the irreversible removal of video data from Loom's servers to comply with privacy and data protection standards. Once a video is deleted, all associated data is purged from the system, meaning the video can no longer be accessed, even with the original link."

On the delete action: "When the video owner chooses to delete a video, it is immediately and permanently deleted." There is no interval in that sentence.

On asking for help: "Because Loom Support can't recover deleted videos, it is recommended to use the archive feature for content you may need later."

## There is no bin to look in

Loom documents no trash, no recently-deleted list and no restore. Pages promising a 30-day Loom trash are describing something Loom does not have, and following them costs you the time you could spend on the one route that works.

The only reversible action is the one you choose in advance. "Archiving hides videos from your main library without purging the data, allowing you to restore them at any time." Archived videos still count towards the free plan's 25, which [Loom's free plan limits](/articles/loom-free-plan-limits) covers.

## Two other ways a video goes for good

Loom documents the same permanence on two admin paths. An admin removing a user "have the option to permanently delete all of their content during the off-boarding process", and if you close your own account "all content owned by them across all of the workspaces they're a member of is permanently deleted".

Tidying up and leaving are the dangerous moments, and both are when people reach for delete. Take copies first: [how to download a Loom video as an MP4](/articles/download-loom-video-mp4) covers one video, and [getting your Loom library out](/articles/export-your-loom-library) covers the rest.

The share link dies with the video, which is the part that catches people out. Somebody on r/atlassian had linked theirs in a job application.

## Nothing to recover

A Prequel recording is a folder on your Mac from the moment you press stop, and an export is a file you chose the place for. Deleting a share link does not touch either: the link stops working and the video sits where it always was. [Where files live](/docs/where-files-live) lists what a session holds, and [share links](/docs/share-links) covers the part that is optional.

Recovery is a question you only have to ask about a copy you never held.
