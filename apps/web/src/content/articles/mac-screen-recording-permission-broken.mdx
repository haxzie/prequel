Every third-party recorder on your Mac has stopped capturing, and the permission looks granted. Apple's own screenshot tool still works, which makes it feel like the apps broke. The permission is the part that broke.

What follows is Apple's documentation where Apple has any, and clearly marked as reported where it does not.

## The setting changed its name

- **macOS 14 Sonoma:** `Privacy & Security` then `Screen Recording`.
- **macOS 15 Sequoia and later:** `Privacy & Security` then `Screen & System Audio Recording`, with Apple's own split of "both your screen and audio, or just your audio".

The rename landed with Sequoia, not with Tahoe, and Apple rewrote its older page to match. So a guide that tells you to look for `Screen Recording` on macOS 15 or later is describing Sonoma. [The screen recording permission](/glossary/screen-recording-permission) covers what it gates.

## macOS asks again on a schedule

Sequoia introduced a recurring consent prompt for screen recording. In the early betas it appeared weekly and on every reboot. Before 15.0 shipped, Apple moved it to monthly and dropped the reboot trigger, and the prompt carries an **Allow For One Month** button.

Apple has never documented any of this: not the prompt, not the cadence, not the change. That is worth knowing, because a dialog nobody can find a support page for reads as malware the first time it appears.

## When it is ticked and the capture is still blank

Apple documents the toggle and the **Add** button for putting an app in the list. It documents no troubleshooting for an app that holds the permission and captures nothing. There is no Apple page for this symptom, so everything below is community practice:

1. **Quit the app first.** The grant is read when it starts.
2. **Turn it off and on again** in the list, then relaunch.
3. **Remove it** with the minus button and let it ask again.
4. **Reset the decision.** `tccutil reset ScreenCapture <bundle-id>`. Apple's `man tccutil` documents `reset` and the bundle argument, and does not list the service names, so the `ScreenCapture` part is community knowledge.
5. **Restart the Mac**, which is the step people skip and the one that most often works.

Apple does document that the grant is matched against a designated code requirement as well as a bundle id or path, which is why a re-signed or relocated build can arrive as a stranger.

Somebody on r/MacOS had Kap, Screen Studio and Snipaste all stop at once while QuickTime kept working, and reported that removing and re-adding them did not help and that it came back some time after each restart. [Read the thread on r/MacOS](https://www.reddit.com/r/MacOS/comments/1tup9io/all_of_the_screen_recording_and_screenshot_tools/).

## What the built-in tools can and cannot do

Worth knowing before you decide the third-party app was the problem. On macOS 14, 15 and Tahoe 26, `Cmd+Shift+5` records the microphone and not the Mac's own sound: Apple's **Include System Audio** option arrives in macOS 27. QuickTime Player's documented options list a microphone only, at every version. **Record Selected Window** is new in Tahoe 26, so before that the built-in tool could not record a single window.

If internal sound is what you are missing, [system audio](/glossary/system-audio) explains the term and [why Tella does not record system audio](/articles/tella-system-audio-not-recording) covers the same wall in another app. [What to check before you press record](/articles/before-you-press-record) is the five-minute version of all of this, and [why your camera is missing from a Loom recording](/articles/loom-camera-not-showing) is the camera's version of the same permission story.
