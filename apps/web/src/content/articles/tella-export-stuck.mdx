An export that stops at 97% is a bad end to an edit. Tella's help centre has a page for it, which says how common it is.

## What Tella describes

The page is titled "Export or download fails, errors out, or gets stuck", and it names the symptom: "It never finishes: the progress bar gets stuck at a percentage, or the Preparing download window stays open and nothing happens."

## What Tella suggests

Tella's first-line fix is to re-render the video. It has a page called "Re-render a video in Tella", and its page on pops and crackles in audio says a fresh render usually fixes them. You trigger a re-render by making a small edit, so Tella builds a new version.

Beyond that, the usual checks apply:

1. **Check your connection** and keep the tab open until the download starts.
2. **Look in Downloads** and your browser's download list before you run it again.
3. **Try a different browser** if the download window never closes.
4. **Contact support** with the video link if a re-render does not help.

## Why this happens in cloud-first tools

Tella's pages describe a Preparing download step, and its first fix is to re-render the video, which suggests the export is prepared by Tella before it reaches you and not on your Mac. That is a reason exports can fail in ways that have nothing to do with your computer: a stale render, a dropped connection, a download that never arrives. [Why Tella makes you wait before you can edit](/articles/tella-cloud-editing) explains the order of events, and [Loom stuck in processing](/articles/loom-stuck-in-processing) is the same failure in a different tool.

Local recorders have their own export problems, of a different kind. [Cap's export problems](/articles/cap-export-problems) are about exports that fail on specific versions, and [why Screen Studio exports take so long](/articles/screen-studio-export-slow) is about time.

What you can export depends on the plan too: [Tella export limits](/articles/tella-export-limits) has the details, and [Tella's editor](/articles/tella-editor-slow) is the page for the wait before it.

## Exporting on your own Mac

In Prequel, **Export** opens a dialog with the video format, frame rate and quality, and the resulting size. The render runs on the Mac's video hardware, the button fills as it goes, you can keep editing meanwhile, and closing the dialog does not stop it.

When it is done the video plays in the dialog. You can drag it into Slack, Mail or a folder, reveal it in Finder, or copy it to the clipboard. The file is on your disk before anything is shared. See [export](/docs/export).
