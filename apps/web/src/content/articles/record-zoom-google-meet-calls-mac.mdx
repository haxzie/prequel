The reason people go looking for this is always the same: a call worth
keeping, and a Mac that recorded the screen and their own voice and none of
what the other person said. QuickTime and the Cmd+Shift+5 toolbar record a
microphone. The other side of a call comes out of the speakers, and macOS does
not hand that to either of them.

There are three ways to get both sides, and which one to use depends on
whether you control the meeting. Then there is the part nobody in those
threads mentions until the comments: say you are recording.

## Tell the call you are recording

Do this first, whichever route you take. Zoom and Meet both announce their
own recording to everyone on the call, which is one reason people prefer them.
A screen recorder announces nothing. Say it out loud at the start and, for a
client, put it in the invite. In some places consent from every party is the
law; everywhere, it is the difference between a recording you can use and one
you cannot.

## 1. Use the meeting's own recorder if you are the host

**Zoom** records to your Mac with **Record** in the toolbar, on the free plan
as well as the paid ones, as long as you are the host or the host has allowed
you to. The file lands in your Documents folder under Zoom as an MP4 when the
meeting ends, with everyone's audio mixed together.

**Google Meet** has a **Record meeting** option too, but only on paid
Workspace plans, only if the administrator has turned it on, and only for the
host or someone the host permits. A personal Google account cannot record a
Meet.

Both give you the gallery or the shared screen as the meeting showed it, one
mixed audio track, and no control over the layout afterwards. For a call you
want to keep, that is fine. For a call you want to edit into something,
the next route gives you more.

## 2. Record the screen with system audio

This is the route the X thread is about. The Mac's own sound, everything
coming out of the speakers, is called [system audio](/glossary/system-audio),
and a recorder either captures it or does not.

The old way, which that thread teaches, is a virtual audio device: install
Background Music or BlackHole, route the Mac's output through it, and point
the recorder at the virtual device as if it were a microphone. It works. It
also means a kernel extension or a driver, an Audio MIDI Setup session, and
a Mac that goes silent when the routing is wrong.

The current way is a recorder built on the screen capture API macOS has had
since version 13, which can hand an app the system's audio alongside the
frames with nothing installed. Prequel is one: **Record system audio** in
Settings is on by default, and the take carries the call's audio as its own
track.

![Settings, with Record system audio on](/docs/settings.webp)

Turn the microphone on too, so your side is recorded, and leave the camera
off unless you want your own face in the recording. Then choose the meeting
window as the source, so the recording is the call and not your desktop.

![The Audio panel: include and volume for the microphone and for system audio](/docs/inspector-audio.webp)

Two tracks is the point. In the editor the other side's audio and yours have
their own **Include** switch and their own level, so a call where you were
too loud or they were too quiet can be balanced afterwards. A meeting
recorder's single mixed track cannot be.

The [internal audio post](/blog/record-mac-screen-with-internal-audio) goes
through which recorders capture system audio and which still need the driver.

## 3. Record each side locally for a podcast

The r/podcasting thread's most upvoted answer is a service that records
every participant's audio on their own machine and uploads it, so a bad
connection does not end up in the recording. That is the right answer for a
weekly show with guests, and it is a subscription and a link the guest has to
open.

For a one-off conversation, route two is the same idea at your end: your
microphone is recorded locally at full quality, and the guest's side is
whatever the call delivered. The difference shows when the guest's connection
drops, and for most calls it does not.

## What to do with the recording

If it was a call worth recording, it is usually a call worth sending part of.
The take opens in Prequel's editor with the zooms placed on anything you
clicked, which for a call is usually nothing, so delete any it found. Cut the
recording down to the part that matters on the timeline, and export at 1080p
in MP4 for a file that plays anywhere, or use **Share Link** to send a URL
instead of a file. The [file size article](/articles/reduce-screen-recording-file-size-mac)
has what to expect from each.

Captions are drawn from the microphone track, so your side of the call is
transcribed on the Mac and can be typed into; see [captions](/docs/captions).

## The short version

Say you are recording. If you host the meeting, Zoom's own Record button is
enough and Meet's is if your plan has it. If you do not, or you want to edit
the result, record the screen with a recorder that captures system audio as
a separate track: Prequel does on any Apple Silicon Mac running macOS 14 or
later, with nothing to install and nothing to route.
