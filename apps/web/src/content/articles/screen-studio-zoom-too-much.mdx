Automatic zoom is the feature people buy a recorder for, and the one a surprising number of them end up turning down. The complaint is the same in every thread: it moves too much.

## What people say

In a Hacker News thread about an open-source Screen Studio alternative, one commenter wrote: "I must be in the minority but I find that constant panning/zooming to be very distracting and almost dizzying. The sharp start of the easing curves is pretty awful too. I'm surprised people like it. I'd probably do it with arrows or fading out parts of the screen instead."

[Read the comment on Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47646924)

A reply from someone who pays for a recorder agreed in part: "I would love the ability to do more highlighting and less panning." They said that in a small video, zooming is necessary to show where the action is, and that recording editors lack arrows, callouts or a freeze frame, so they plan the recording to the letter and sometimes do ten takes.

## Why it feels like too much

A zoom is added for every click. In a demo with 30 clicks that is 30 moves, and the eye follows each one. Three things make a move feel bigger than it is: how far it goes in, how quickly it starts, and how often it repeats.

That gives you three controls to turn down, and the order to do it in.

## How to calm it down in Prequel

Prequel adds zooms on its own the first time a recording opens, one for each place you clicked or typed. Each one is a block in the zoom row under the clips.

![The Prequel timeline with three zoom blocks at 1.6x, 2.0x and 2.1x under the recording](/articles/prequel/zoom-timeline.webp)

Select a zoom and open the **Zoom** tab.

1. **Level.** Lower it. A shallower push is less to follow.
2. **Speed.** Lengthen the push in and pull out. A slow move reads as calm and a fast one as a jolt.
3. **Ease.** Choose **Slow in** or **Slow out**, or drag the curve. This is the "sharp start" that the Hacker News commenter objected to.
4. **Delete the ones you do not need.** Select a zoom and press **Backspace**. Fewer, better-placed zooms are the biggest change.
5. **Follow.** Set it to **Region** to hold a fixed area.

## Or leave the picture where it is

Set **Method** to **Loupe**, and nothing moves. A glass lens comes down over the thing you are pointing at, magnifies it, and leaves the rest of the screen readable. It takes its level, speed and ease from the same tab.

![A Prequel recording with a loupe magnifying part of the screen while the rest stays still](/articles/prequel/zoom-loupe.webp)

The **Glass** tab sets how the lens looks, with curvature, fringing and reflection. The [zooms page](/docs/zooms) lists every control, and [zoom and pan](/glossary/zoom-and-pan) explains the terms.
